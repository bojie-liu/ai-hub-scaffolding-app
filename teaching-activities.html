Teaching & Learning Activities (120 minutes)

All activities are designed with Social Constructivism principles: collaborative knowledge-building, peer negotiation, and contextual comparison.

🎬 Introduction (15 minutes)

Hook Activity: Think-Pair-Share

Visual Prompt: Two contrasting images are shown:

  • Image A: A teacher in a highly regulated system (e.g., Korea)
  • Image B: A teacher in a deregulated system (e.g., charter school)

Question: "Which one looks more professional? Why?"

Format:

  1. Think (1 min): Individual reflection
  2. Pair (2 min): Discuss with a partner
  3. Share (3 min): Whole-class sharing

Pre-Test Review (5 minutes)

Class aggregate results (anonymized) are displayed. Instructor highlights the most common misconception (e.g., "Professionalism is the same everywhere").

Discussion prompt: In pairs, propose why that misconception exists.

Real-World Connection (3 minutes)

News headline: "Teachers in Country X strike for professional autonomy"

Reflection: "How does your understanding of professionalism influence your view on this strike?"

No answer needed yet – just to spark curiosity.

📋 Teaching Tips

  • Use Mentimeter to collect think-pair-share responses in real-time
  • Display pre-test aggregate data using LMS analytics dashboard
  • This activates prior knowledge and sets a collaborative tone

🎨 Development Activities (85 minutes)

Activity 1: Jigsaw Reading / Expert Groups (25 minutes)

Divide class into 5 mixed groups (5 students each). Each group becomes expert on one aspect of teacher professionalism:

Group A: Professional Knowledge & Standards ▼

Source: Hargreaves & Fullan - Professional Capital

Focus: What constitutes professional knowledge? How are standards defined?

Task: Create a Google Jamboard slide summarizing key points, one example, and one question for other groups.

Group B: Autonomy and Accountability ▼

Source: Finland/USA article

Focus: How do different systems balance teacher autonomy with accountability?

Task: Compare contrasting approaches and create Jamboard summary.

Group C: Ethical Codes and Conduct ▼

Source: Local context (Hong Kong or Singapore code of practice)

Focus: What ethical principles guide professional conduct?

Task: Identify key ethical standards and cultural influences.

Group D: Professional Development & Career Progression ▼

Source: OECD TALIS data

Focus: How do teachers develop professionally across their careers?

Task: Map career pathways in different systems.

Group E: Teacher Identity & Status ▼

Source: Comparative (Japan vs. Australia)

Focus: How is teacher status constructed socially and culturally?

Task: Analyze factors influencing teacher identity.

🌐 How to Use Google Jamboard

  1. Go to jamboard.google.com
  2. Click "Create new Jam" or use the shared class link
  3. Your group will work on one designated slide/frame
  4. Use sticky notes to add key points (click the sticky note icon)
  5. Add images or drawings to illustrate concepts
  6. Collaborate in real-time – all group members can edit simultaneously
  7. Include: Key Points, One Example, One Question

Activity 2: Cross-Pollination - Gallery Walk (20 minutes)

Groups rotate to view other Jamboards (2 minutes per stop). Use sticky notes (physical or digital) to add:

  • One "aha!" moment – something that surprised or enlightened you
  • One "I wonder" – a question or area for further exploration

Return to home group: Share findings and integrate across topics.

Whole-class debrief (5 min): "What patterns emerged? Where did you see contradictions?"

📱 Use Padlet for Digital Sticky Notes

  1. Access padlet.com using the class link
  2. Each group's Jamboard has a corresponding Padlet column
  3. Double-click to add a new sticky note
  4. Label your note with "Aha!" or "I wonder"
  5. Include your group letter (A-E) for tracking

Activity 3: Case Study – A Professionalism Dilemma (25 minutes)

📋 Case Scenario

Ms. Lam's Dilemma:

Ms. Lam, a new teacher in an international school in Hong Kong, is asked by her principal to adopt a scripted curriculum to ensure standardisation across grade levels. However, she feels it undermines her professional judgment and reduces her ability to respond to students' diverse needs.

Meanwhile, a colleague from Finland says that in their home country, teachers have almost complete autonomy over pedagogy.

Task (in pairs): Use the conceptual map you have built to analyze the dilemma. Discuss:

  1. What are the competing definitions of professionalism at play?
  2. How do local vs. international contexts shape expectations?
  3. What would you advise Ms. Lam, and why?

Synthesis: Each pair writes a one-paragraph recommendation. Three volunteers read theirs aloud; class votes on the most context-sensitive advice.

📊 Use Mentimeter for Voting

  1. Go to menti.com
  2. Enter the code displayed by your instructor
  3. Vote for the recommendation that best balances context, autonomy, and accountability
  4. View results in real-time as a class

Activity 4: Collaborative Concept Mapping (15 minutes)

Using a large shared whiteboard (Miro or physical whiteboard), the whole class constructs a master concept map capturing the dimensions of teacher professionalism in local and international contexts.

Starting point: Central node = "Teacher Professionalism"

Branch out to:

  • Standards & Ethics
  • Autonomy & Accountability
  • Professional Development
  • Status & Identity
  • Contextual Influences

Task: Students add specific examples from their expert groups and case discussion.

🗺️ How to Use Miro for Concept Mapping

  1. Access miro.com using the shared class board link
  2. Find the central node labeled "Teacher Professionalism"
  3. Use the sticky note tool to add examples and concepts
  4. Draw connections between related ideas using the connector tool
  5. Color-code by context: Blue for local, Green for international
  6. Add your initials to your contributions for tracking

🎯 Synthesis & Closure (20 minutes)

Post-Test (7 minutes)

Complete the same 5 MCQ as the pre-test, plus two open-ended reflection questions. This is available in the Assessment section.

Peer-Teaching Summaries (8 minutes)

In groups of 3: Each student explains one key insight from the lesson to their peers.

Group task: Select the most "powerful" insight to share with the whole class.

📝 Share Insights on Padlet

  1. Each group posts their selected insight on the class Padlet
  2. Title your post with your group number
  3. Write 2-3 sentences explaining the insight
  4. Read other groups' insights and add reactions
