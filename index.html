Professional Studies · Foundations of Teaching

Teacher Professionalism in Local and International Contexts

Teacher professionalism is one of the most contested ideas in education — and one of the most frequently misrepresented by AI. In this 150-minute social-constructivist seminar, Year 2–3 pre-service teachers compare how professionalism is understood and regulated across six systems (Hong Kong SAR, Mainland China, Finland, Singapore, Japan, England–USA), then audit confident AI-generated accounts of those systems against primary sources. AI appears in every section — not as an answer engine but as an object of critical scrutiny that students must verify, revise or reject.

Section 1

Intended Learning Outcomes

Each outcome states what students will do and how AI is engineered into it — as material to analyse, verify and override rather than to trust.

ILODescription🤖 AI Integration
ILO 1Analyse the concept of teacher professionalism using a multi-dimensional framework (professional knowledge, autonomy, ethical commitment, self-regulation, service ethic) and explain how each dimension manifests differently across local (Hong Kong / Greater China) and international (Finland, Singapore, Japan, England–USA) contexts.The pre-class AI Probe Log supplies a first, unverified account that the Segment 1 jigsaw then corrects against primary sources — students see the AI version dismantled by evidence.
ILO 2Critically evaluate how policy discourses — managerialism, performativity, standards-based reform and audit culture — shape, constrain or enable teachers' professional autonomy and identity in comparative settings.The Bias & Framing Audit (Segment 3, Pass 3) applies the same critical lens to AI discourse as to policy discourse, so students learn to hear the normative assumptions in both.
ILO 3Apply comparative frameworks to authentic professionalism dilemmas (social-media conduct, mandated assessment, ethical disclosure about a colleague, culturally contested norms) and justify a defensible course of action with reference to both local codes of conduct and international principles.Dilemmas D1 and D6 directly involve AI-mediated practice, requiring students to apply professionalism reasoning to the governance of AI itself.
ILO 4Collaboratively construct a context-sensitive Professionalism Charter for a designated education system, synthesising evidence, peer critique and comparative analysis into a coherent, defensible position.AI may be used to draft the Charter — but the Audit Log must document every accepted, modified and rejected suggestion with a rationale and a primary source.
ILO 5Critically evaluate AI-generated accounts of teacher professionalism for factual accuracy, currency and bias — including Global-North/Anglophone bias and the homogenisation of culturally distinct traditions — identifying hallucinations and overgeneralisation.This is the core activity: the four-pass AI Audit runs source, accuracy, bias and judgement passes against primary sources, then a hostile-read exchange makes peer scrutiny a group norm.
ILO 6Exercise and document independent judgement in deciding when, whether and how to act on AI-generated content — articulating explicitly which AI suggestions were accepted, modified or rejected, and why human judgement took precedence.The Accept / Modify / Reject log, the Override Record and the closing reflection discussion make independent judgement visible, named and socially valued rather than private.
🤖 Design principle

AI output is treated as a draft hypothesis, never a source. A claim without a traceable primary source is unverified, not true; a confident tone is not evidence of accuracy, because fluency and fabrication are produced by the same mechanism. Students must be able to defend, under questioning, any AI suggestion they accept — and to document every suggestion they modify or reject.

Section 3

Teaching & Learning Activities

The run sheet moves from a provocative scenario to collaborative knowledge production: a six-system comparative jigsaw, a scaffolded interactive lecture on policy discourses, a four-pass AI Audit, ill-structured dilemma work, and a calibrated gallery walk. AI touchpoints are marked throughout.

1 Round 1 — Comparative Jigsaw — Distributed Expertise across Six Systems

Home groups (6 groups of 15) are each assigned one system: Hong Kong SAR, Mainland China, Finland, Singapore, Japan, or England–USA. Each group splits into three expert triads — Triad A: Qualification & Knowledge Base; Triad B: Regulation, Ethics & Accountability; Triad C: Autonomy, CPD & Career. Expert triads populate a shared Padlet comparative matrix (Dimension / Local System / Evidence (source) / Degree of autonomy 1–5 / Notes) from the Source Pack, then return to the home group and teach their dimension, producing a consolidated system profile on a shared slide. No group can complete the profile without all three triads, so participation is structurally required rather than merely encouraged. (25 minutes; the AI Critique Lab and simulated chat below are used in later segments.)

🎓 Instructor guidance on critical AI use
  • AI output is a draft hypothesis, never a source. A claim without a traceable primary source is unverified, not true.
  • Confident tone is not evidence of accuracy — fluency and fabrication are produced by the same mechanism.
  • Your professional obligation is to know the system you will work in. Delegating that knowledge to an AI is a professional development failure, not an efficiency.
  • You may accept AI output — but only after verification, and you must be able to defend it under questioning.
  • When AI feedback arrives on your own drafting it is advisory and pre-moderated: the instructor remains the sole authority for grading, and any AI comment that misstates the rubric or applies an Anglophone default is removed before you see it.

Section 4

Assessment Methods

Assessment is layered: low-stakes formative checks that make thinking visible during the session, and a summative Comparative Professionalism Portfolio that includes a non-compensable Critical AI Engagement appendix.

🎯 Post-test 14 items

Fourteen items: twelve re-sequence and re-contextualise the pre-test constructs (trait theory, the Finnish model, performativity, the Sachs binary, the semi-profession thesis, Hong Kong conduct guidance, AI limitations, verifying AI claims, the Evans distinction, Hargreaves' ages, professional capital and activist professionalism), and two transfer items extend them to policy diffusion and framework universality — so growth is measured against the same misconception categories.

Section 7

Differentiation & Inclusivity

Multiple entry points, scaffolds and AI-supported accommodations so that every student can contribute knowledge to the jigsaw — with extension pathways for advanced learners.

♿ Support
  • Multiple jigsaw entry points: the three expert triads (Qualification / Regulation / Autonomy) let policy readers, ethics-oriented and practice-oriented students each enter from a strength.
  • Scaffolded Source Packs: each system's pack includes a one-page orientation summary for students with less prior comparative knowledge.
  • Peer mentoring: triads deliberately mix students with and without international experience; those with lived experience act as cultural informants, acknowledged by the instructor as an epistemic contribution.
  • Bilingual glossary of key terms (performativity, managerialism, professionality, semi-profession) in English, 繁體中文 and the other languages represented in the cohort.
  • AI-supported scaffolding labelled 'AI-generated, instructor-reviewed': simplified explanations and step-by-step walkthroughs of the Sachs binary, so students always know the status of the material.
  • Accessibility: captioned videos (AI captions reviewed before release), tagged accessible PDFs with a dyslexia-friendly font option, dilemma cards in print / digital / audio, and a digital Padlet gallery walk for students with mobility constraints.
  • Extended time and alternative formats for the AI Audit Log — an audio recording is accepted in place of a written log.
  • A five-minute optional 'AI basics' clinic before the session for students with limited AI experience.
🚀 Extension
  • Lead the Tier 3 dilemmas (D5 — a colleague's out-of-school conduct; D6 — teacher voice on an AI-driven evaluation committee) and serve as gallery-walk discussants rather than presenters.
  • Undertake a second AI audit on a Global South or Indigenous education system outside the Source Pack, and report the AI's representational gaps.
  • Design the group's Charter with an explicit implementation and resourcing plan, including anticipated opposition.
  • Take the Chair role in the Segment 3 hostile read, synthesising critique rather than producing it; and simulate a managerialist-versus-activist policy debate with AI, then critique the simulation's straw-man tendencies.
