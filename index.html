Activity Block 2: Case Study Jigsaw

⏰ 35 minutes

Objective: Analyze three professional dilemmas across different contexts using the Jigsaw protocol

The Three Case Studies:

Case 1 | Local Urban School

Grade Inflation Pressure

Ms. Chen teaches mathematics at an urban high school. At the end of the semester, her principal calls her into the office and pressures her to "reconsider" the failing grades she assigned to several students, suggesting that the school's performance metrics will suffer. The principal hints that her contract renewal might depend on "being a team player." Ms. Chen believes her grades accurately reflect student performance and that inflating them would compromise her professional integrity. However, she needs this job and worries about the students' futures if they fail.

Dilemma: Should professional autonomy override institutional pressure? What standards are at stake?

Case 2 | International School Abroad

Cultural Discipline Clash

Mr. Rodriguez, from Spain, teaches at an international school in Singapore. He uses a collaborative, informal approach to classroom management that worked well in Europe. However, parents complain that he is "too lenient" and doesn't enforce strict discipline, which conflicts with local expectations for teacher authority. The school administration suggests he adopt more traditional, hierarchical methods. Mr. Rodriguez feels this would undermine his pedagogical beliefs about student agency and democratic classrooms, but he's also new to this cultural context.

Dilemma: How do culturally different views of authority shape teacher professionalism? Should he adapt or advocate?

Case 3 | Online Teaching Environment

Digital Surveillance vs. Academic Integrity

Dr. Patel teaches an online university course. During exams, the university's proctoring software monitors students' screens and webcams, flagging "suspicious" behavior. Dr. Patel reviews the flags and finds many false positives—students looking away to think, accessing permitted notes, or having family members in the background. The software disproportionately flags international students in small apartments. Dr. Patel questions whether this surveillance is professional or ethical, but the university insists it's necessary to maintain academic integrity in online settings.

Dilemma: Does professionalism require prioritizing academic integrity over student privacy? What are the equity implications?

Jigsaw Protocol:

1
Home Groups

Original groups of 4-5. Each member becomes "expert" on one case (5 min reading)

2
Expert Groups

All students with same case meet. Discuss dilemmas, define standards violated, propose solutions (10 min)

3
Return to Home

Each expert teaches their case & solution (3 min each = 12 min total)

4
Synthesize

Group identifies common professionalism principles across cases (8 min)

Activity Block 3: Collaborative Definition Construction

⏰ 25 minutes

Objective: The whole class collectively constructs one sentence definition of teacher professionalism that is context-sensitive yet robust

Process:

  1. Post Principles (5 min): Each home group posts their synthesized list of common principles on the board
  2. Structured Debate (10 min): Facilitate discussion: "Which principle is most contested across contexts?" (e.g., autonomy vs. accountability)
  3. Collective Writing (7 min): Using the emerging list, the class constructs ONE sentence definition
  4. Peer Critique (3 min): "Does this definition work for all three case contexts? What might a teacher in [country X] add?"
📄

Digital Tool: Google Docs for Co-Construction

How to Use Google Docs for Collaborative Writing
  1. Access the shared Google Doc via the link provided by your instructor
  2. The instructor will share editing access with all students
  3. Groups contribute their principles in bullet points at the top
  4. As the class discusses, the instructor (or designated scribe) drafts the definition in real-time
  5. Students can use Suggesting mode to propose edits to the definition
  6. Use the comment feature to flag concerns or propose alternatives
  7. Watch the definition evolve collaboratively in real-time
  8. Final version is saved and shared with all students

Purpose: Real-time collaborative writing makes the negotiation of meaning visible and participatory.
