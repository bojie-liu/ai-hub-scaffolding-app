Duration: 90 minutes
Level: Second-year undergraduates
Class Size: 30 students
Pedagogy: Social Constructivism

Lesson Overview

Pedagogical Framework: Social Constructivism

This lesson treats knowledge about professionalism as socially negotiated, culturally situated, and co-constructed through dialogue, comparative inquiry, and collaborative problem-solving.

Course Context

  • Course: Foundations of Education / Teacher Professionalism
  • Topic: Teacher Professionalism in Local and International Contexts

Constructive Alignment Principle

All outcomes, teaching activities, and assessments in this lesson reinforce one another. Students engage in authentic tasks that mirror the complexity of real professional contexts.

Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)

By the end of this 90-minute lesson, students will be able to:

  • ILO 1: Analyze competing definitions of teacher professionalism in local and international policy contexts using collaborative inquiry. (Analyze; Social Constructivism: co-construction of meaning)
  • ILO 2: Compare and contrast at least two professional standards/frameworks (e.g., UNESCO, OECD-TALIS, national/local standards) and identify the contextual values embedded in each. (Analyze/Evaluate; Social Constructivism: multiple perspectives)
  • ILO 3: Evaluate ethical dilemmas in teacher professionalism through structured dialogue, weighing local cultural expectations against international professional norms. (Evaluate; Social Constructivism: dialogic negotiation)
  • ILO 4: Create a collaboratively produced "Contextually Responsive Professionalism Charter" that synthesizes international principles and local realities. (Create; Social Constructivism: collective knowledge production)

Pre-Class Preparation

Note: These are preparatory social-constructivist tasks, not full flipped learning. Students engage with materials to activate prior knowledge before collaborative inquiry begins.

Diagnostic Pre-Test Quiz ▼

Tool: Mentimeter or Moodle Quiz with adaptive feedback

How to Set Up the Quiz:

  1. Visit Mentimeter and create a free account
  2. Create a new presentation and add 5-7 quiz questions
  3. For each question, enable the "Show correct answer" feature
  4. Add adaptive feedback explanations for common misconceptions
  5. Share the quiz code with students via LMS before class

Example Quiz Items:

  1. Statement: "Professionalism is mainly about having a teaching qualification."
    Adaptive feedback if "Agree": "Many people think this, but qualifications are only one dimension. In this course, we explore professionalism as practice, ethics, and context."
  2. Statement: "International teacher standards should always override local cultural norms."
    Adaptive feedback if "Agree": "This is a common tension. In our session, we will examine when and why such an assumption may be problematic."
  3. Statement: "Teacher professionalism is an individual achievement rather than a collective responsibility."
    Adaptive feedback if "Agree": "Social constructivist views of professionalism emphasize collegial dialogue, school culture, and shared norms."
  4. Statement: "Professionalism looks the same in every country."
    Adaptive feedback if "Disagree": "Good! Your experience may already reflect the importance of local context."

Pre-Class Guiding Questions

Post these on the LMS discussion forum before class:

  1. What does "professionalism" mean in your local educational context? Where do those meanings come from—policy, culture, history, media, teachers themselves?
  2. Find one real-world example of a teacher professionalism dilemma (news story, personal experience, film) that could be viewed differently in two cultural or national contexts.
  3. Bring a question about professionalism that you hope the class will help you answer.

Teaching & Learning Activities (90 minutes)

15 minutes

Introduction

3.1 Pedagogy-Aligned Hook (5 min) ▼

Tool: Mentimeter Poll

Setup Instructions:

  1. Create a new Mentimeter poll with the question: "A truly professional teacher should follow the same ethical and professional standards anywhere in the world."
  2. Add three options: Agree / Disagree / Not sure
  3. Display results in real-time as students vote
  4. After voting, students do a quick think-pair-share with a partner

Discussion Prompts:

  • "Why did you choose this option?"
  • "What experience or assumption influenced you?"
Social Constructivism Note: The hook introduces divergent perspectives and creates cognitive dissonance—an essential starting point for co-constructed inquiry.
3.2 Pre-Test Review with Misconception Analysis (7 min) ▼

Display the diagnostic quiz results anonymously. Highlight common misconceptions:

  • "Professionalism is just compliance with rules."
  • "International frameworks are neutral and culture-free."
  • "Local context is a barrier to professionalism, not a resource."

Activity: In pairs, students challenge or refine one statement using examples from their own lives.

3.3 Real-World Connection (3 min) ▼
Scenario: "A teacher who trained in one country moves to an international school in another country. Students' parents expect frequent communication and gifts; the school's code of conduct forbids gifts and limits teacher–parent contact. How should the teacher act?"

Explain that this lesson will give students conceptual tools to understand and respond to such dilemmas.

60 minutes

Development Activities

Activity 1: Social Constructivist Jigsaw (20 min) ▼

Tool: Padlet for collaborative mapping

Setup Instructions:

  1. Create a free Padlet board before class
  2. Set up 5 columns, one for each expert group (A through E)
  3. Share the Padlet link with students
  4. Divide students into 5 expert groups (6 students each)

Expert Group Assignments:

Group Source / Focus
Group A Local professional standards / code of ethics
Group B OECD-TALIS conceptualisation of teacher professionalism
Group C UNESCO Recommendation concerning the Status of Teachers
Group D Hargreaves' "Four Ages of Professionalism"
Group E Case study of an international school's framework

Expert Group Task:

Each expert group reads key sections and answers:

  1. What core values are emphasised?
  2. What contextual assumptions are embedded in this framework?
  3. What would this framework say about the "gift-giving" dilemma?

Groups record findings on the Padlet board using sticky notes, images, or short audio messages.

Home Groups (Jigsaw):

Re-group into "home groups" with one member from each expert group. Each student teaches their framework to others, using this graphic organiser:

Framework Core Values Contextual Assumptions Implications for Dilemma
Fill in collaboratively during home group discussion
Social Constructivism Note: Jigsaw methodology creates interdependence: students cannot complete the task without hearing, questioning, and building on each other's expertise.
Activity 2: Ethical Dilemma Carousel (25 min) ▼

Format: Structured Controversy Protocol with station rotation

Setup Instructions:

  1. Place 4 large chart papers around the classroom, each with one dilemma
  2. Divide the class into 4 groups (7-8 students per group)
  3. Each group visits all 4 stations (7 minutes per station)
  4. Provide markers for groups to write on chart paper

Dilemma Stations:

Station 1: Social Media Boundaries

A teacher in an international school is "befriended" by a student's parent on Facebook. In his home culture, this is friendly and normal. In the school's international code, it is a professional boundary violation.

Station 2: Assessment and Grading

A local norm values effort and improvement; international school policy requires objective final-examination grading. A teacher must decide whether to "round up" a culturally respectful but low-performing student.

Station 3: Teacher Appearance

A female teacher wears culturally traditional dress that is conservative in her own community, but the international school handbook requires "Western professional attire."

Station 4: Parent Gifts

In many local communities, giving gifts to teachers is a sign of respect. International codes often prohibit accepting gifts to avoid favouritism.

Task at Each Station:

  • Articulate both sides of the dilemma fairly
  • Discuss what "professionalism" means to each stakeholder
  • Write a negotiated position on the chart paper, including at least one unresolved tension

Teacher Facilitation Prompts:

  • "What would the international framework say? What would the local community say?"
  • "Is there a third possibility that respects both perspectives?"
  • "Whose voice is missing from this dilemma?"
Social Constructivism Note: Structured controversy encourages students to co-construct more complex judgments rather than simply defending their own initial view.
Activity 3: Building a Shared Professionalism Charter (15 min) ▼

Tool: Google Slides or Padlet

Setup Instructions:

  1. Create a shared Google Slides presentation with one slide per group
  2. Share edit access with all students
  3. Each group gets 10 minutes to draft their charter on their assigned slide
  4. Conduct a 5-minute gallery walk where groups add sticky note comments to other slides

Task:

Using insights from Activities 1 and 2, each group drafts 3–5 "Contextually Responsive Professional Commitments." These should:

  • Integrate at least one international principle (e.g., integrity, equity, accountability)
  • Respect at least one local cultural value or expectation
  • Be worded as practical commitments teachers could enact

Gallery Walk Protocol:

  1. Groups post their charter draft on Google Slides or Padlet
  2. Class conducts a gallery walk, placing sticky notes with questions or suggestions
  3. Groups briefly respond to one key question or suggestion
Social Constructivism Note: The final "charter" is an authentic shared product—constructed through discussion, critique, and consensus-building.
15 minutes

Synthesis & Closure

Post-Test & Belief Re-Evaluation (5 min) ▼

Repeat the opening Mentimeter poll:

"A truly professional teacher should follow the same ethical and professional standards anywhere in the world."

Compare the new distribution with the pre-class responses. Discuss:

  • What changed? What remained the same?
  • Did any new tensions emerge?
Peer Teaching Summaries (5 min) ▼

Each group shares one "aha" insight and one unresolved question. Encourage classmates to respond.

Example prompts:

  • "One thing we now understand differently is…"
  • "One tension we still need to explore is…"
Exit Ticket & Preview (5 min) ▼

Tool: Google Forms or paper exit ticket

Exit Ticket Prompt:

"In 2–3 sentences: How has your understanding of teacher professionalism changed? What question will you take into the next session?"

Preview Next Session:

"Next time, we will apply these frameworks to teacher evaluation systems and continuous professional development in local and international schools."

Summative Assessment

Group Digital Portfolio: "Teacher Professionalism in Context" ▼

Due: One week after the lesson

Components:

  1. Comparative framework analysis (500–700 words)
    • Compares local standards with one international framework (OECD/UNESCO) using concepts from Activity 1
  2. Case response (300–400 words)
    • Selects one carousel dilemma and formulates a justified, contextually responsive professional response
  3. Professionalism Charter (from Activity 3)
    • Polished version with a short rationale for each commitment
Individual Reflective Essay (300–500 words) ▼

Prompt: "In what ways did dialogue with peers challenge or enrich your understanding of teacher professionalism?"

Differentiation & Inclusivity

Social-Constructivist Supports

  • Multiple entry points: Students can contribute to Padlet via text, audio, or video; oral participation is not the only way to show understanding
  • Group roles: Assign rotating roles (timekeeper, note-taker, synthesizer, devil's advocate, reporter) so all students can participate meaningfully
  • Scaffolded sources: Expert group materials are available in simplified and extended versions, depending on student need
Extension for Advanced Learners ▼
  • Challenge advanced students to compare an additional international framework (e.g., Australia's AITSL standards, Finland's teacher education principles) during the jigsaw
  • Ask them to draft a professional development workshop proposal based on their professionalism charter
  • Invite them to analyze how social media has changed the public perception of teacher professionalism
Accessibility Accommodations ▼
  • All videos/images captioned and described
  • PDF readings screen-reader accessible
  • Large-print and high-contrast versions of case cards
  • Flexible seating and movement for mobility devices
  • Additional processing time for individual reflection and exit tickets
  • Option to submit audio or video responses instead of written text

Reflection & Improvement

Pedagogy-Specific Success Indicators

  • Dialogue quality: Did students listen, question, and build on peers' ideas? Use an observation checklist
  • Conceptual nuance: Did post-test responses show increased complexity and less binary thinking?
  • Collaborative output: Did the group charter reflect multiple perspectives rather than one dominant voice?

Student Feedback (3-2-1 Format)

  • 3 ideas you learned about professionalism
  • 2 ways group discussion helped or hindered your understanding
  • 1 question you still have

Modification Strategies

  • If students struggle with abstract international frameworks, replace one reading with a video case study of a teacher working across cultures
  • If local context is underrepresented, invite a guest teacher or school leader to join the dilemma carousel and share their lived experience
  • If time is limited, reduce the carousel from four stations to two and provide more structured scaffolds for each station
