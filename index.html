Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)

By the end of this session, students will be able to:

  • Analyze the key dimensions of teacher professionalism as defined in local (e.g., Hong Kong) and international (e.g., OECD, UNESCO) frameworks, identifying tensions and convergences.
  • Evaluate the influence of cultural, political, and economic factors on conceptions of teacher professionalism using comparative case studies.
  • Synthesize a personal definition of professionalism that integrates multiple perspectives and demonstrates critical awareness of context.
  • Critically interrogate AI-generated summaries of policy documents on teacher professionalism, identifying biases, omissions, and limitations in the outputs.
  • Decide when and how to rely on AI tools versus independent judgment when analyzing professional standards, with documented rationale.

Bloom's Taxonomy Levels

This lesson targets higher-order thinking skills: Analyze, Evaluate, Synthesize, Critically interrogate, and Decide (for AI literacy).

Pre-Class Preparation

📚 Pre-Reading Materials

  • Sachs, J. (2016) – "Teacher Professionalism: Changing Discourses in a Globalized World" (excerpt)
  • Hong Kong Education Bureau (2020) – "Teacher Professional Standards" (summary)

🎯 Diagnostic Quiz

Complete this 5-question quiz before class to assess your baseline understanding:

Question 1: Which of the following is NOT a characteristic of teacher professionalism according to the OECD?

A. Specialized knowledge and skills
B. Autonomy in professional practice
C. Guaranteed lifetime employment
D. Commitment to ethical standards

Question 2: Teacher professionalism in East Asian contexts often emphasizes:

A. Individual autonomy above all
B. Moral responsibility and community service
C. Market-driven performance only
D. Rejection of professional standards

Question 3: Which framework provides international comparative data on teaching and learning?

A. PISA only
B. TALIS (Teaching and Learning International Survey)
C. World Bank Education Index
D. National Assessment Programs

💭 Guiding Questions

Reflect on these questions as you complete your reading:

  • What tensions exist between Western notions of teacher autonomy and East Asian emphasis on moral responsibility?
  • How do you think AI would define teacher professionalism? What might it miss?

🤖 Pre-Class AI Engagement Task

Before class, interact with an AI tool (ChatGPT or Claude):

Prompt to use: "Summarize the key elements of teacher professionalism in the OECD Teaching and Learning International Survey (TALIS) framework."

Reflection prompt: "What strengths and limitations do you see in the AI-generated summary? Note one point you disagree with or find incomplete."

Teaching & Learning Activities

Provocative Hook

Statement: "Teacher professionalism is a Western construct imposed on the rest of the world."

Students use Mentimeter to indicate agreement/disagreement.

How to use Mentimeter:
  1. Instructor creates account at mentimeter.com
  2. Create a "Scales" question with the statement
  3. Display unique code for students to join via phones
  4. Students visit menti.com and enter code
  5. View live results as students respond

AI-Generated Scenario

Show two AI-generated paragraphs:

  • Finland: High autonomy focus
  • Shanghai: Accountability focus

Critical prompt: "Which shows stronger evidence? What biases might AI have introduced?"

Real-World Connection

Recent news about teacher conduct in local vs. international schools.

Think-Pair-Share (15 minutes)

Individual (5 min): Write 3–5 core elements of teacher professionalism using pre-reading.

Pair (5 min): Compare with partner; add AI-generated definition as third perspective.

Share (5 min): Instructor collects responses on Padlet.

How to use Padlet:
  1. Create account at padlet.com
  2. Choose "Wall" format for brainstorming
  3. Create two sections: "Local" and "International"
  4. Share Padlet link or QR code with students
  5. Students add definitions as sticky notes
  6. Organize responses in real-time

AI-Enabled Critical Activity (10 minutes)

AI Statement: "Teacher professionalism is universally defined by qualifications, ethical codes, and continuous professional development."

Small groups (3–4 students) critique:

  • What cultural assumptions are embedded?
  • How would this change if AI had been trained on African or South American education systems?
  • List one aspect AI missed about local contexts (e.g., Hong Kong's emphasis on serving the community).

Case Study Groups

Groups of 5–6 students each receive one case study:

Group Case Study Focus Country/Region
1 High autonomy, trust-based Finland
2 Performativity and examination pressure Shanghai, China
3 Community-oriented, resource-scarce Kenya
4 Market-driven reforms Chile
5 Hybrid model (British + Chinese) Hong Kong

Step 1: AI Profiling (15 minutes)

Students read 1-page case handout and use AI to generate a professionalism profile of their country.

Task: "Prompt AI to list top 3 characteristics of teacher professionalism in your case country. Compare with handout. Where does AI over-generalize or rely on stereotypes? Revise the AI output to be more culturally accurate. Document your changes."

Step 2: Jigsaw Activity (20 minutes)

Reshuffle groups so each new group has one member from each case study. Each expert presents their case and their critique of AI's portrayal. Group uses shared Padlet to identify common themes and unique variations.

Step 3: Full Class Synthesis (15 minutes)

Each group shares one key tension. Instructor records on whiteboard using TPACK-like diagram: Culture × Policy × AI Representation.

Elevator Pitch Task (20 minutes)

In pairs, prepare a 2-minute elevator pitch: "How would you define teacher professionalism for a new international school in Hong Kong that hires teachers from both local and overseas backgrounds?"

AI component: First ask ChatGPT to draft a definition. Then annotate the draft: underline parts you accept, strike through parts you reject, and add comments explaining why human judgment overrode AI.

Peer Feedback (10 minutes)

Each pair presents to another pair. Listeners use critical evaluation checklist:

  • Did the presenter question their own assumptions?
  • Did they account for both local and international expectations?
  • How did they justify overriding AI suggestions?

Post-Test (2 minutes)

Same 5-question quiz as pre-class; compare results quickly to show improvement.

AI Reflection Discussion (5 minutes)

Instructor poses:

  • One moment where AI misled you about teacher professionalism – how did you realize?
  • One moment where AI helped you see a new angle.
  • What does this tell you about using AI in education policy analysis?

Preview (3 minutes)

Next session: "Teacher Professionalism in Action: Ethics and AI in the Classroom" – bring your annotated AI outputs.

Assessment Methods

Formative Assessment (During Class)

  • Observations: Instructor uses structured checklist to note quality of critique during AI interrogation (e.g., identifying bias, asking counterexamples).
  • Group collaboration: Depth of comparative analysis.
  • Real-time quizzes: Poll responses and Padlet contributions.
  • AI-Supported Formative Checks: Students submit annotated AI-generated professionalism profile via Moodle assignment. Instructor (or AI tool) provides feedback on critical thinking demonstrated.

Summative Assessment

Task: Individual written reflection (800–1000 words) due one week later:

"Critically evaluate the role of AI in shaping understanding of teacher professionalism across contexts. Using at least two case countries and your own AI interactions from class, argue whether AI tools can ever adequately represent the complexity of local professionalism. Where must human judgment take precedence?"

Assessment Rubric

Criterion (Weight) Excellent (4 pts) Proficient (3 pts) Developing (2 pts)
Comparison of local & intl frameworks (25%) Insightful synthesis of at least two cases with nuanced tension analysis Clear comparison with specific examples Some comparison but lacks depth
Critical AI Literacy (30%) Actively interrogates AI outputs; provides concrete examples of bias/limitations and justifies overrides Evaluates AI outputs but less consistently Mentions AI but no deep critique
Personal synthesis & judgment (25%) Creative, well-argued personal definition showing independent critical thought Good personal stance but limited evidence Definition present but largely rehashes sources
Use of evidence & documentation (20%) References class AI interactions and pre-class tasks; transparent about AI use Relevant evidence but could be more thorough Some evidence but lacks AI transparency

AI Use Log Requirement

Students must include an appendix: "AI Use Log" – detailing prompts used, AI outputs, and what they accepted/modified/rejected with reasons.

Grading emphasis on quality of critical engagement with AI, not volume of AI use.

Differentiation & Inclusivity

👥 For Struggling Learners

  • Pre-class AI task: Includes scaffolded prompt template
  • In-class support: AI can simplify case study language (text-to-speech available)
  • One-on-one AI coaching: For students unfamiliar with prompting
  • Extended time: Additional 5 minutes for activities

🚀 For Advanced Learners

  • Extension activity: "Critique AI's representation using postcolonial theory"
  • Leadership role: Serve as "AI auditors" for deeper bias checking
  • Additional research: Compare AI representations across different models

♿ Accessibility Accommodations

  • Visual: All videos captioned, slides in high contrast
  • Hearing: AI-generated transcripts of class discussion available
  • Motor: Students can submit elevator pitch as audio recording
  • Cognitive: Provide case study summaries in bullet-point format

🌍 Linguistic & Cultural Support

  • Language support: AI can translate key terms into mother tongue
  • Multilingual AI interaction: Students can query AI in preferred language
  • Cultural context: Case studies represent diverse global contexts
