📚 Lesson Overview

Pedagogical Framework: This lesson uses Social Constructivism as its central pedagogical framework. Knowledge about professionalism is treated as socially negotiated, culturally situated, and co-constructed through dialogue, comparative inquiry, and collaborative problem-solving.

Course Context

Course: Foundations of Education / Teacher Professionalism

Topic: Teacher Professionalism in Local and International Contexts

Design Principles

  • Constructive Alignment: Outcomes, teaching activities, and assessments reinforce one another
  • Dialogue & Negotiation: Knowledge co-constructed through structured conversation
  • Cultural Context: Professional standards viewed as culturally situated, not universal
  • Authentic Tasks: Real-world dilemmas and collaborative charter creation

🎯 Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)

By the end of this 90-minute lesson, students will be able to:

  • ILO 1 - Analyze: Analyze competing definitions of teacher professionalism in local and international policy contexts using collaborative inquiry.
    Social Constructivism: co-construction of meaning
  • ILO 2 - Compare: Compare and contrast at least two professional standards/frameworks (e.g., UNESCO, OECD-TALIS, national/local standards) and identify the contextual values embedded in each.
    Social Constructivism: multiple perspectives
  • ILO 3 - Evaluate: Evaluate ethical dilemmas in teacher professionalism through structured dialogue, weighing local cultural expectations against international professional norms.
    Social Constructivism: dialogic negotiation
  • ILO 4 - Create: Create a collaboratively produced "Contextually Responsive Professionalism Charter" that synthesizes international principles and local realities.
    Social Constructivism: collective knowledge production

  1. What does "professionalism" mean in your local educational context? Where do those meanings come from—policy, culture, history, media, teachers themselves?
  2. Find one real-world example of a teacher professionalism dilemma (news story, personal experience, film) that could be viewed differently in two cultural or national contexts.
  3. Bring a question about professionalism that you hope the class will help you answer.

Real-World Scenario

Dilemma: A teacher who trained in one country moves to an international school in another country. Students' parents expect frequent communication and gifts; the school's code of conduct forbids gifts and limits teacher–parent contact. How should the teacher act?

Four Dilemma Stations

Dilemma 1: Social Media Boundaries
A teacher in an international school is "befriended" by a student's parent on Facebook. In his home culture, this is friendly and normal. In the school's international code, it is a professional boundary violation.
Dilemma 2: Assessment and Grading
A local norm values effort and improvement; international school policy requires objective final-examination grading. A teacher must decide whether to "round up" a culturally respectful but low-performing student.
Dilemma 3: Teacher Appearance
A female teacher wears culturally traditional dress that is conservative in her own community, but the international school handbook requires "Western professional attire."
Dilemma 4: Parent Gifts
In many local communities, giving gifts to teachers is a sign of respect. International codes often prohibit accepting gifts to avoid favouritism.

Structured Controversy Protocol (7 min per station):

  1. Articulate both sides of the dilemma fairly
  2. Discuss what "professionalism" means to each stakeholder
  3. Write a negotiated position on chart paper, including unresolved tensions

Collaborative Charter Creation

Task: Draft 3–5 "Contextually Responsive Professional Commitments"

Requirements:

  • Integrate at least one international principle (integrity, equity, accountability)
  • Respect at least one local cultural value
  • Word as practical commitments teachers could enact

Summative Assessment

Due: One week after the lesson

Components:

  1. Comparative framework analysis (500–700 words)
    Compare local standards with one international framework (OECD/UNESCO) using concepts from Activity 1.
  2. Case response (300–400 words)
    Select one carousel dilemma and formulate a justified, contextually responsive professional response.
  3. Professionalism Charter (from Activity 3)
    Polished version with a short rationale for each commitment.

Prompt: "In what ways did dialogue with peers challenge or enrich your understanding of teacher professionalism?"

Summative Rubric (Excerpt)

Criteria Excellent Developing Needs Revision
Contextual awareness Nuanced analysis of both local and international perspectives; avoids stereotypes Acknowledges context but tends to generalize Ignores or simplifies context
Use of frameworks Skillfully applies UNESCO/OECD/local frameworks to cases Mentions frameworks but superficially No reference to course materials
Collaboration / co-construction Clear evidence of shared thinking, negotiation, and peer contribution Some evidence of collaboration Individual work only
Critical reflection Shows complexity, self-questioning, and growth Some reflection, mostly descriptive Does not address learning process
