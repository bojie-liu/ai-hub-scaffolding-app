Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)

Bloom's Taxonomy levels noted

  • 1
    Analyse

    Analyse how teacher professionalism is defined and operationalised in at least one local and one international context, identifying underlying cultural, historical, and policy influences.

  • 2
    Critically Evaluate

    Critically evaluate the tensions between managerial accountability and professional autonomy, using examples drawn from contrasting education systems.

  • 3
    Apply

    Apply frameworks of teacher professionalism to construct contextually sensitive, ethically justified responses to authentic professional dilemmas.

  • 4
    Co-Create

    Co-create a collaborative synthesis of "non-negotiable" aspects of professional practice that bridges local and international expectations, using peer critique to refine the group's work.

  • 5
    Reflect / Evaluate

    Reflect on their own emerging professional identity, recognising how global and local forces can shape values, assumptions, and classroom practice.

Constructive Alignment Note These outcomes directly shape the design of the pre-reading, in-class collaborative tasks, formative checks, and summative assessments detailed in this plan.

Segment 2 – Local vs International: A Jigsaw Exploration

40 – 80 minutes (40 min)

⏱ Activity Timer – Segment 2 (40 min) 40:00 40:00
Goal: Compare how different contexts define teacher professionalism through structured cooperative enquiry.

Set-Up 5 min

Divide 60 students into four expert groups (~15 per group). Each group receives 2–3 short primary-source excerpt cards.

Group A – Nordic
Finland
  • Research-based professionalism
  • High teacher autonomy
  • Master's-level preparation
  • Minimal external inspection
Group B – Anglophone / Standards-Based
England or United States
  • External professional standards
  • Performance appraisal systems
  • Career stages / pay scales
  • Ofsted / accountability culture
Group C – East Asian
Shanghai or Singapore
  • Moral responsibility emphasis
  • Strong collaborative culture
  • High societal expectations
  • Teacher-as-scholar tradition
Group D – International Organisations
UNESCO & OECD TALIS
  • ILO/UNESCO 1966 Recommendation
  • OECD TALIS 2018 findings
  • Global benchmarks & indicators
  • Teacher autonomy vs policy pressure

Expert Group Work 15 min

Each expert group reads their excerpts and completes the graphic organiser:

Dimension Group A – Nordic Group B – Anglophone Group C – East Asian Group D – International
Key values underlying professionalism
Expected roles & responsibilities
Balance: autonomy vs accountability
How "professional knowledge" is defined

Distribute as a printed template or shared digital document.

Mixed-Group Sharing 12 min

1

Regroup

Rearrange students into mixed groups of four — one expert from each context (A, B, C, D).

2

Expert Sharing (3 min per expert)

Each expert presents their context. Peers fill in the corresponding column of the graphic organiser as they listen.

Whole-Class Debrief 8 min

💬
Question 1

"What do these contexts share? What differs fundamentally?"

🌐
Question 2

"Is there a single 'global professionalism' or only local versions?"

⚖️
Question 3

"How might an international benchmark encourage — or limit — local professional agency?"

📚 Pedagogy Link: Jigsaw is a highly structured cooperative learning strategy that forces interpersonal and conceptual negotiation — Social Constructivism made tangible. Each student holds knowledge others lack, creating genuine interdependence.

Segment 3 – Dilemma Case Studies: Professionalism in Action

80 – 110 minutes (30 min)

⏱ Activity Timer – Segment 3 (30 min) 30:00 30:00
Goal: Apply professional judgement to messy, context-rich dilemmas that resist simple answers.

Decision-Making Scaffold 5 min

Form groups of 4–5. Use this scaffold to analyse your assigned case:

  • 1
    What is the dilemma?
  • 2
    Who are the stakeholders?
  • 3
    What professional knowledge and values apply?
  • 4
    What are the contextual constraints?
  • 5
    What would be your professional response? Why?
  • 6
    What if local and international expectations conflict?

Case Studies Group Work – 15 min

Case A – Tradition vs. International Critique

A respected local teacher uses rote memorisation as a core instructional strategy. An international trainer criticises the approach as "unprofessional" and "outdated."

Key tension: Cultural relevance of pedagogy vs. international "best practice" norms.

Case B – Narrow vs. Broad Professionalism

A teacher believes professionalism is defined solely by classroom practice and refuses to participate in school community events, parental meetings, or peer observation.

Key tension: Minimal compliance vs. extended professional responsibility.

Case C – Accountability vs. Autonomy

A school uses standardised test results as the primary basis for reprimanding teachers. A committed teacher experiences loss of autonomy and declining morale.

Key tension: Managerial professionalism vs. democratic/autonomous professionalism.

Case D – Global Curriculum vs. Local Culture

An international NGO requires teachers to use a prescribed "canned" curriculum that ignores local cultural contexts, languages, and community values.

Key tension: Donor compliance vs. professional responsibility to learners.

Each group prepares a 2-minute "professional opinion" or role-play. Groups should challenge each other's assumptions rather than agree too quickly.

Gallery Walk – PMI Protocol 10 min

Groups post responses on large paper or a digital board. Peers rotate and leave PMI feedback:

➕ Plus

What is strong or persuasive?

➖ Minus

What is missing or problematic?

💡 Interesting

What further question does this raise?

J
Jamboard (or Miro / Physical Posters) – Gallery Walk Board
Digital whiteboard for posting case responses and PMI peer feedback
As an alternative to Jamboard, use Miro or Mural (both free tiers available), or physical A3 posters.
  1. Open jamboard.google.com (or Miro) and create one frame/area per case study.
  2. Label each frame: Case A | Case B | Case C | Case D.
  3. Share the board link with "Anyone can edit" access.
  4. Groups post their 2-minute summary using sticky notes on their case's frame.
  5. Set a 2-minute timer; groups rotate to the next frame and add PMI notes: green = Plus, red = Minus, yellow = Interesting.
  6. Repeat until all groups have reviewed all cases.
  7. Instructor highlights 2–3 stand-out feedback notes during the wrap-up.
Learning Purpose: PMI protocol encourages substantive critique rather than surface-level agreement, making diverse perspectives visible to the whole class.
📚 Pedagogy Link: Authentic ill-structured problems require learners to negotiate meaning, take perspectives, and justify decisions — the core mechanism of constructivist learning.

Segment 4 – Collaborative Synthesis & Peer Critique

110 – 125 minutes (15 min)

⏱ Activity Timer – Segment 4 (15 min) 15:00 15:00
Goal: Move from case application to broader professional principles that bridge local and international contexts.

Task – "Five Non-Negotiables" 10 min

In groups of 4–5, develop Five Non-Negotiables for Teacher Professionalism valid in both local and international contexts. Each must include a one-sentence justification. One representative posts on Padlet.

Example structure (students develop their own):

  • Commitment to ongoing learning

    The knowledge base of teaching evolves; a professional who stops learning cannot serve students equitably.

  • Ethical responsibility to learners

    Students are in a position of trust; the teacher's moral agency shapes not just outcomes but who each student becomes.

  • Contextual responsiveness

    Universal prescriptions ignore the cultural, linguistic, and material realities of diverse classrooms.

  • Collaborative professional practice

    No teacher can improve in isolation; peer learning is a structural requirement of sustained quality.

  • Balanced accountability and autonomy

    External oversight without professional agency produces compliance, not genuine improvement.

P
Padlet – Non-Negotiables Synthesis Board
Groups post their Five Non-Negotiables for whole-class comparison
  1. Create a new Padlet at padlet.com titled "Our Non-Negotiables for Teacher Professionalism".
  2. Choose the Shelf layout — one column per group.
  3. One representative from each group posts their five principles as a numbered list.
  4. Display the Padlet on the projector for whole-class comparison.
  5. Identify principles appearing across multiple groups (consensus) vs. those appearing only once (productive divergence).
  6. Use divergent principles as the starting point for the hot-seat critique.
Learning Purpose: Seeing all groups' syntheses simultaneously enables meta-level reflection on shared values and generative disagreements.

Peer Critique – Hot Seat Protocol 5 min

1

One group presents

A representative reads their Non-Negotiables and explains the reasoning behind the most contested one.

2

Class asks clarifying questions

"What did you mean by X? How would this work in context Y? What would you cut if you could only keep three?"

3

Respectful critical thinking

Focus on intellectual challenge, not winning. The goal is to surface tensions and refine thinking through dialogue.

Formative Observation: Instructor circulates, noting groups that demonstrate strong dialogue, equal participation, and evidence of genuinely challenging ideas — not just sharing.
📚 Pedagogy Link: Collaborative creation and peer assessment are the apex of Social Constructivist pedagogy — knowledge is actively built, tested, and refined through critical social interaction.

Differentiation & Inclusivity

Strategies to support all learners in a class of 60

📚 Multiple Formats

  • Reading, video, and audio versions of key sources provided
  • Supports varied learning preferences and accessibility needs
  • All videos include captions; materials can be enlarged or digitally adapted
  • Mentimeter accessible on mobile devices — no laptop required

🤝 Group Roles

  • Each group assigns four roles to support co-construction and equal participation:
  • Facilitator — keeps discussion on track
  • Recorder — takes notes on graphic organiser
  • Reporter — presents to the whole class
  • Timekeeper — monitors allocated time

🗣️ Language Supports

Sentence stems are provided for multilingual learners and students building academic confidence:

"One example from my context is…"
"I notice a tension between…"
"In my reading, I found that…"
"This context is similar to / different from mine because…"
"I would argue that… because…"

🚀 Extension for Advanced Students

  • During discussions, prompt with higher-order questions:
  • "How might your proposed solution be critiqued by a different cultural perspective?"
  • "What ideological assumptions does this framework embed?"
  • "Could two of your Non-Negotiables contradict each other — in what circumstances?"

🧩 Cognitive Load Management

  • Session broken into clear 15–25 minute segments with frequent transitions
  • Graphic organiser structures information processing during jigsaw
  • Decision-making scaffold (6 questions) breaks dilemma analysis into steps
  • PMI protocol structures peer feedback to avoid cognitive overload

♿ Accessibility

  • All videos include closed captions
  • Digital materials can be enlarged or adapted for visual impairments
  • Mentimeter/Padlet accessible via mobile — reduces hardware barriers
  • Physical poster alternatives for students who prefer offline participation
  • Flexible summative assessment: written essay or recorded oral presentation with transcript

Modification Strategies for Next Time

Evidence-based adaptations based on reflection

1

If students struggled with the international perspective

Spend more pre-class time scaffolding the UNESCO/TALIS excerpts. Consider adding a brief annotation guide that directs students to key passages and prompts them to note one surprising finding per reading.

2

If some groups moved through activities very quickly

Invite them to develop a brief counter-argument to their Non-Negotiables: "What is the strongest case against each of your five principles? What evidence would undermine it?"

3

If time is limited

The dilemma case activity can be extended into a full standalone lesson using the "step into the lobby" peer-review protocol. Alternatively, assign case analysis as asynchronous preparation and use class time for the gallery walk only.

4

If participation was uneven

Implement a "round-robin" share before the whole-class debrief to ensure all voices are heard before the discussion is dominated by confident speakers. Consider anonymous Padlet posting for reticent students.

Session Summary

The big picture

This 150-minute seminar uses Social Constructivism to help students investigate teacher professionalism as a contested, context-dependent concept. Through pre-reading, diagnostic questioning, jigsaw analysis, case-based problem-solving, collaborative synthesis, and peer critique, students move from personal assumptions to nuanced professional understanding — ready to carry this critical sensitivity into their own teaching practice.
