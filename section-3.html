Section 3a — Introduction Activities

PSY101 · 0–15 minutes · 12% of lesson time · Social Constructivism + AI Integration
🎤 Instructor Opening Script
"Good morning, everyone. Today we're diving into learning theories — but we're doing something different than a traditional lecture. Today you'll work together as psychologists-in-training, and you'll also treat AI as a tool, a resource, and sometimes, a trap. By the end of this class, I want you to be able to answer three questions: (1) What distinguishes classical, operant, and observational learning? (2) How do these theories apply to real life? (3) When should you trust AI, and when should you challenge it?"
⏱Introduction Timeline (0–15 min)
0–5 min
🐶 Provocative Hook: "The Conditioned Classroom"

Students enter class and see a slide pairing a puppy image with the school logo. Instructor asks: "Without saying anything — notice your emotional reaction. What did you feel? Why might I have shown you this combination?"

Students write a one-word reaction on a sticky note, then share with a partner.

Pair-Share
Sticky Notes

Social Constructivism: Activates prior knowledge; pair discussion surfaces patterns of similar/different responses, beginning collaborative sense-making.

5–8 min
🤖 AI-Enhanced Focus: Critical Evaluation of AI Examples

Instructor reveals the puppy + logo setup as a conditioning demonstration (US = puppy, NS = logo). Then models critical AI interrogation:

"I asked an AI to generate 5 examples of classical conditioning in everyday life. Let's see them — and critically evaluate them as a class."

Display 5 AI-generated examples. Students raise thumbs up/down on accuracy.

🤖 AI Integration Focus

Instructor transparently demonstrates their own critical evaluation of AI outputs. Students participate as judges — establishing the class norm that AI outputs are assessable, not authoritative.

8–12 min
📋 Pre-Test & AI Journal Discussion

Instructor displays the 5 pre-test questions and reveals correct answers. Students discuss in pairs:

"Did anyone get Q2 wrong? What made it tricky? Did your AI journal reveal any similar misconceptions?"

Instructor calls on 3 pairs to share AI-related insights from their pre-class journal.

Social Constructivism: Peer discussion targets misconception resolution within ZPD — students learn from each other's errors through discourse.

🤖 AI Journal Share

Students share observations such as: "The AI's explanation helped me understand the distinction I got wrong" or "The AI confused extinction and punishment — I caught it."

12–15 min
🗺 Connection Preview & ILO Introduction

Instructor states the 7 ILOs and frames the lesson:

"Today we'll build knowledge together, critically examine AI as a learning tool, and prepare for our group synthesis project."

Explicitly frames AI literacy: "You'll become critical consumers of AI — learning when AI helps and when your judgment must prevail."

📊External Tool: Mentimeter — Pre-Test & Live Polls

Purpose: Run the 5-question diagnostic and live thumbs-up/down poll on AI examples. Results display instantly and drive pair-share discussions.

▶ How to use Mentimeter in this lesson
  1. Go to mentimeter.com and sign in (free account sufficient).
  2. Click New Presentation → choose Quiz for the 5 pre-test questions.
  3. Add each question with multiple-choice options; mark the correct answer for automatic scoring.
  4. Add a second slide as Scales / Rating for the AI example thumbs-up/down activity.
  5. Share the join code on the classroom projector. Students join on their phones.
  6. Advance slides manually; results populate live for whole-class discussion.
  7. Export results after class via the dashboard for your teaching reflection journal.

🤝Social Constructivism Links — Introduction Phase
  • Pair-Share (0–5 min): Activates personal schemas; surfaces shared/divergent reactions as starting points for meaning-making.
  • Class AI judgment (5–8 min): Instructor models critical interrogation; students collectively assess AI accuracy — establishing collaborative norms.
  • Peer misconception correction (8–12 min): Learning through discourse within ZPD; peers help each other resolve pre-test errors.
  • Explicit framing (12–15 min): "We will learn with and from each other" — foregrounding collaborative knowledge-building for the whole lesson.
