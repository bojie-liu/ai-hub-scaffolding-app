PSY101 · ~20 minutes independent study · Complete before the lesson

📚2.1 Required Pre-Reading & Media

Complete all three items before class. They activate the schemas you'll need for group activities.

📖Textbook Reading

Schacter, Gilbert & Wegner (2020). Psychology (3rd ed.). Worth Publishers.
Chapter 7: Learning — Sections on Classical & Operant Conditioning.

Focus: understand the vocabulary (US, UR, CS, CR, reinforcement, punishment) before class.

🎬Khan Academy Video

Classical Conditioning (2014) — Khan Academy MCAT series.

~10 min. Pause and rewind when the US/CS distinction is introduced.

📺Crash Course Psychology

How to Train a Brain (2014) — Crash Course Psychology #11.

~10 min. Covers classical and operant conditioning with memorable examples.

💬2.3 Pre-Class Guiding Questions

Write 2–3 sentence responses and bring them to class. These seed your group discussions.

1. Real-World Connection ▼

Reflect on an advertisement you've seen that pairs a product (neutral stimulus) with an appealing image (unconditioned stimulus). Explain how this reflects classical conditioning.

Social Constructivism link: Activates personal schemas as the starting point for collaborative meaning-making in class.

2. Cultural Perspective ▼

Based on your cultural background, share an example of how learning occurs through observation of elders or role models. How does this reflect observational learning theory?

Social Constructivism link: Frames learning theories within cultural diversity — critical for Vygotsky's emphasis on sociocultural context.

3. Curiosity Question ▼

Pose one question about learning theories that you hope the class session will answer. Write it down — you'll share it during the introduction activity.

Social Constructivism link: Student-generated questions drive peer inquiry during class.

🤖2.4 Pre-Class AI Engagement Task
🎯 Purpose

Build AI literacy before class. Arrive with critical impressions of AI capabilities and limitations ready to share with your group.

Time: ~15 minutes  |  Tools: ChatGPT / Claude / Copilot

💬AI Prompt to Use
"You are a psychology tutor. Please explain the difference between classical conditioning and operant conditioning, provide two examples of each, and indicate whether the following scenario is classical or operant: 'A student studies hard to avoid getting a bad grade.' Also, identify one limitation of each theory."
📝Guided Reflection Journal (2 paragraphs)

Document your answers to these prompts. Bring your journal to class — it will be used in the Introduction activity.

1. What did the AI explain accurately? ▼

What did you already know that confirmed the AI's explanation? Where did your prior knowledge align with the AI output?

2. Where was the AI incomplete or misleading? ▼

Did the explanation seem oversimplified? Were any nuances missing? Did the AI confuse extinction with punishment, or miss schedules of reinforcement?

3. Did you find any cultural biases? ▼

Did the AI's examples all come from certain cultural contexts? Did it force examples into a Western framework? Consider whether the examples are universally relatable.

4. How would YOU explain it differently? ▼

If you were the tutor, how would your explanation differ? What personal examples or culturally relevant scenarios would you use?

5. What would human expertise add? ▼

What can a human psychologist or instructor provide that AI cannot? Consider: nuance, ethical judgment, lived experience, cultural competence.

✅Pre-Class Checklist
  • Read Chapter 7 (Classical & Operant Conditioning sections)
  • Watch Khan Academy Classical Conditioning video (~10 min)
  • Watch Crash Course Psychology #11 (~10 min)
  • Complete the 5-question diagnostic quiz above
  • Write 2–3 sentence responses to each guiding question
  • Complete the 15-minute AI engagement task and write 2-paragraph journal
