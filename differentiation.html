🌈 Differentiation & Inclusivity

This lesson embeds Universal Design for Learning (UDL) principles alongside ZPD-aligned differentiation to ensure all 90 students can engage meaningfully.

ZPD-Aligned Differentiation

Mixed-Ability Grouping

Groups are formed using diagnostic quiz data to ensure each group contains a range of proficiency levels. Less confident readers receive peer scaffolding; stronger readers are challenged to articulate their thinking clearly.

Tiered Text Options (Text B)

Three to four versions of Text B are prepared at different complexity levels. All groups complete the same annotation task, but with appropriately challenging source texts.

  • Option 1: Narrative / short story with clear structure
  • Option 2: Opinion piece with moderate inference demands
  • Option 3: Adapted academic text with complex syntax

Role Cards with Sentence Starters

Each role card includes sentence starters to lower the language barrier for participation:

Leader

  • "Let's hear from everyone before we decide…"
  • "We have 5 minutes left — let's focus on…"

Clarifier

  • "Could you show me where you saw that?"
  • "What made you write a question mark here?"

Connector

  • "This reminds me of the Gibbons reading when…"
  • "I annotated this because…"

Scribe

  • "I'll record: our puzzling phrase is…"
  • "Our group's key insight was…"

Reporter

  • "At first we thought ___, but after discussion we now think ___ because ___."

General Starters

  • "This makes me wonder…"
  • "I thought X, but now I think Y because…"

Extensions for Advanced Groups

  • Draft a two-minute mini think-aloud script for a new, unseen text
  • Evaluate the level of challenge in Text B using ZPD language
  • Create a "scaffold-free" version of the annotation task for more independent learners
  • Take on the role of peer coach, supporting another group's annotation process

Accessibility & Inclusivity Measures

  • Captioned videos: All pre-class videos include closed captions; digital text is screen-reader compatible
  • High-contrast slides: Dark text on white background; minimum 24pt font on classroom-projected materials
  • L1 clarification: Judicious use of first language permitted for complex vocabulary, especially during initial text reading
  • Multiple modalities: Visual (annotation cards), audio (think-aloud), and kinesthetic (sticky note annotation) options provided
  • TA support: Teaching assistants circulate with targeted help for groups identified as needing additional scaffolding
  • Flexible annotation: Physical sticky notes or digital Padlet accepted — no single mode required
