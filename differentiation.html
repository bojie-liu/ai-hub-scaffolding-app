🤝 Support for Students Who Need Extra Help

These scaffolds help students who are not yet ready for abstract thinking:

  • Place value mats with colour-coded labels: Hundreds = blue, Tens = green, Ones = red (matching base-10 block colours)
  • Reduced number range early in lesson (up to 500 instead of 999)
  • Colour-coded number cards to highlight digit positions
  • Strategic pairing with peer mentors who can verbally model reasoning
  • Continued use of base-10 blocks through comparison activity (not rushed to abstract)

Key Principle: Allow extra time in concrete stage. Some students need weeks with manipulatives before transitioning to pictorial. Don't force premature abstraction.

🚀 Extension for Advanced Learners

If you finish activities early and want more challenge:

  • Work with 4-digit numbers (1,000-2,000) including harder renaming problems ("How many tens are in 1,350?")
  • Explore regrouping: "What must we do if we have 10 ones? What if we have 10 tens?"
  • Design your own "Fair Challenge" scenario with self-chosen numbers
  • Play online: Place Value Baseball or Numbers to 1,000

Note: Challenge with depth (e.g., "explain three different ways to show 456"), not just more of the same. Advanced learners benefit from open-ended problems.

♿ Accessibility Accommodations

This lesson is designed to be accessible to all learners:

Visual Supports

  • Captioned videos for pre-class material
  • Large-print worksheets and high-contrast place value charts
  • Colour-coded materials with distinct, high-contrast colours

Kinesthetic & Motor Supports

  • Extra time with physical base-10 blocks for kinesthetic learners
  • Magnetic/digital base-10 representations on interactive whiteboard for fine motor difficulties
  • Chunky markers and grips for students with limited dexterity

Multisensory Instruction

  • Verbal + visual instructions together
  • Repeat key vocabulary with accompanying gestures (flatten hands for "hundreds", make sticks with fingers for "tens")
  • Audio descriptions of visual materials

Universal Design Principle: Design activities so all students can participate meaningfully, regardless of ability. Multiple means of representation, engagement, and expression built into every activity.

💬 If You Need Help

Everyone learns at their own pace! If you need help:

  • Ask your teacher for coloured place value mats
  • Work with a partner who can explain in your own words
  • Use blocks for as long as you need — there's no rush!
  • Ask questions anytime — your teacher is here to help
