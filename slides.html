Introduction to Learning Theories Slide 3 / 10

Behaviourism

Key Assumptions

  • Observable behaviour only
  • Stimulus–response relationships
  • Reinforcement & punishment shape behaviour
  • Blank slate (tabula rasa)

Key Theorists

Watson, Pavlov, Skinner, Thorndike

Classroom Applications

  • Drills & repetition
  • Reward systems
  • Immediate feedback
  • Programmed instruction

Example

Skinner's operant conditioning: positive reinforcement in homework management systems increases desired behaviours.

Famous Quote

"Give me a dozen healthy infants... and I'll guarantee to take any one at random and train him to become any type of specialist..."

— J.B. Watson

Introduction to Learning Theories Slide 4 / 10

Cognitivism

Key Assumptions

  • Mental processes matter
  • Information processing model
  • Schema theory — organising knowledge
  • Cognitive load limits learning

Key Theorists

Piaget (early), Bruner, Ausubel, Sweller, Gagné

Classroom Applications

  • Advance organisers
  • Mnemonics & chunking
  • Worked examples
  • Graphic organisers

Example

Ausubel's meaningful learning: connect new concepts to existing schema (advance organisers) to improve retention.

Contrast with Behaviourism

Internal mental states are valid objects of study — we can investigate memory, attention, and reasoning.

Introduction to Learning Theories Slide 5 / 10

Constructivism

Key Assumptions

  • Learners actively construct knowledge
  • Prior knowledge is central
  • Social interaction is key (Vygotsky)
  • Learning is contextual & situated

Key Theorists

Piaget, Vygotsky, Bruner, Dewey

Classroom Applications

  • Problem-based learning
  • Collaborative tasks
  • Authentic activities
  • ZPD scaffolding

Social Constructivism (Vygotsky)

Knowledge is co-constructed through dialogue. Learning happens first between people (inter-psychological), then within (intra-psychological).

⚠️ Note: Constructivism DOES build on prior knowledge — counter to a common AI-generated misconception we will examine in class.

Introduction to Learning Theories Slide 6 / 10

Three Theories at a Glance

🔵 Behaviourism

  • Learner as passive recipient
  • Teacher as authority
  • Drill, reward, test
  • Watson, Skinner

🟣 Cognitivism

  • Learner as information processor
  • Teacher as organiser
  • Schema building, chunking
  • Piaget, Ausubel

🟢 Constructivism

  • Learner as active meaning-maker
  • Teacher as facilitator
  • Collaboration, problem-solving
  • Vygotsky, Dewey
Introduction to Learning Theories Slide 7 / 10

Learning by Doing Together

What This Means in Practice

  • Knowledge is built through dialogue & collaboration
  • Jigsaw activity: learning by teaching others
  • Jamboard/Miro: co-constructing a shared framework
  • Peer feedback: validating and refining understanding

Zone of Proximal Development

What you can do with support today vs. what you can do alone — the gap is where learning happens.

"What a child can do in cooperation today, he can do alone tomorrow."

— Lev Vygotsky

Introduction to Learning Theories Slide 8 / 10

AI as a Learning Tool — Handle with Care

✅ Opportunities

  • Generate examples quickly
  • Scaffold explanations
  • Support brainstorming
  • Personalised practice

⚠️ Risks

  • Hallucination
  • Oversimplification
  • Hidden bias
  • Over-reliance
  • Lack of disciplinary nuance

Key message: AI should supplement, not replace, human reasoning. The "AI literacy" imperative: evaluate → modify → override.
