# Lesson Plan: The Accounting Equation - From Street Vendors to Skyscrapers

---

## Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)

By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:

1. **ILO1**: Define and explain the fundamental accounting equation (Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity)
2. **ILO2**: Classify business transactions into their correct elements (assets, liabilities, owner's equity)
3. **ILO3**: Analyze how different types of transactions affect the accounting equation across business scales
4. **ILO4**: Differentiate between capital expenditures and operating expenses in real-world Hong Kong contexts
5. **ILO5**: Apply the accounting equation to solve basic transaction analysis problems

## Teaching Activities

### Introduction - Hook Activity

**Hook Activity**: "In the documentary, Cathay paid HK$20M for new business class seats. How did this affect:
1. Their assets?
2. Their liabilities?
3. Owner's equity?"

### Segment 1: The Accounting Equation Explained

**Interactive Lecture**: Visual comparison between:
- Cathay Pacific's $200M aircraft upgrades (Capital Asset) vs.
- Tai Kok Tsui dumpling cart's $1,200 wok replacement (Operating Expense)

**Guided Discussion**: "Why does Cathay record this as an asset while the street vendor treats similar purchases as expenses? Consider business scale and asset lifespan."

**Key Concepts**:
- Assets = Liabilities + Owner's Equity
- Every transaction affects at least two elements
- The equation must always balance
- Different business scales lead to different accounting treatments

### Segment 2: Transaction Analysis in Practice

**Activity**: Students analyze real Hong Kong business scenarios:

1. **Bubble Tea Stall (Kowloon City)**:
   - Owner invests HK$50,000 personal savings
   - Takes bank loan of HK$30,000
   - Purchases equipment for HK$20,000
   - Buys ingredients for HK$5,000 (cash)
   - Earns HK$8,000 revenue (first week)

2. **Cathay Pacific**:
   - Leases aircraft for HK$500M (operating lease)
   - Purchases spare parts inventory for HK$10M
   - Pays employee salaries HK$200M
   - Receives prepayment for flights HK$50M

3. **Tung Wah Group (Non-Profit)**:
   - Receives donation HK$1M
   - Purchases medical equipment HK$300K
   - Pays staff salaries HK$200K
   - Records restricted fund HK$500K

**Worked Example**:

| Transaction | Assets | Liabilities | Owner's Equity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner invests HK$50K | +HK$50K (Cash) | No change | +HK$50K (Capital) |
| Bank loan HK$30K | +HK$30K (Cash) | +HK$30K (Loan) | No change |
| Buy equipment HK$20K | +HK$20K (Equip) / -HK$20K (Cash) | No change | No change |
| Buy ingredients HK$5K | -HK$5K (Cash) | No change | -HK$5K (Expense) |
| Revenue HK$8K | +HK$8K (Cash) | No change | +HK$8K (Revenue) |

### Segment 3: Capital Expenditure vs Operating Expense

**Comparison Table**:

| Transaction | Effect on Dumpling Cart | Effect on Cathay Pacific |
|---|---|---|
| Buying kitchen equipment | Immediate expense (short term) | Capitalized as asset (long-term) |
| Receiving prepayment | Liability (needs delivery) | Same classification path |
| Purchasing vehicle | Expense if small van | Capital asset if fleet vehicle |
| Staff salaries | Operating expense | Operating expense |
| Major renovation | Expense (low value) | Capitalized (adds value to property) |

**Key Principle**: Materiality and useful life determine classification. If an item provides benefit beyond one year and exceeds a materiality threshold, it is capitalized. Otherwise, it is expensed.

### Segment 4: The Dual Effect Principle

**Interactive Demonstration**: Using the bubble tea stall example, show how every transaction has a dual effect:

1. Investment: Cash increases AND Owner's Equity increases
2. Loan: Cash increases AND Liabilities increase
3. Equipment purchase: Equipment increases AND Cash decreases
4. Revenue: Cash increases AND Owner's Equity increases
5. Expense: Cash decreases AND Owner's Equity decreases

**Student Practice**: Students create their own transaction tables for a lemonade stand business.

## Simplified Financial Statement Impact Examples

### New Scenario Table

| Transaction | Effect on Dumpling Cart | Effect on Cathay Pacific |
|---|---|---|
| Buying kitchen equipment | Immediate expense (short term) | Capitalized as asset (long-term) |
| Receiving prepayment | Liability (needs delivery) | Same classification path |
| Purchasing vehicle | Expense if small van | Capital asset if fleet vehicle |
| Staff salaries | Operating expense | Operating expense |
| Major renovation | Expense (low value) | Capitalized (adds value to property) |

### Balance Sheet Impact

**Dumpling Cart (After 1 month)**:
- Assets: Cash HK$63,000 + Equipment HK$20,000 = HK$83,000
- Liabilities: Bank Loan HK$30,000
- Owner's Equity: Capital HK$50,000 + Revenue HK$8,000 - Expenses HK$5,000 = HK$53,000
- Check: HK$83,000 = HK$30,000 + HK$53,000 ✓

**Cathay Pacific (Simplified, After transactions)**:
- Assets: Cash + Aircraft + Inventory + Prepayments
- Liabilities: Loans + Advance ticket sales + Salaries payable
- Owner's Equity: Share capital + Retained earnings

## Assessment Methods

### Formative Assessment

- **Concept Checks**: Quick thumbs-up/down during each segment
- **Discussion Questions**: Guided discussions with peer feedback
- **Practice Problems**: In-class transaction analysis exercises

### Summative Assessment

- **Case Study**: Analyze Hong Kong Openrice.com's recent transactions:
  - Purchased servers for $500k (Capital Asset)
  - Paid $50k deposit to expand office space (Prepaid Expense)

- **Quiz**: Multiple choice, true/false, and short answer questions covering all ILOs

- **Rubric**:
  - +15% for explaining why identical transaction types (e.g., equipment purchases) might be treated differently by SMEs vs. corporations

## Differentiation

### Language Support
- Include Putonghua subtitles for Cantonese translations on key concepts
- Key accounting terms provided in both English and Chinese:
  - Assets = 资产 (zīchǎn)
  - Liabilities = 负债 (fùzhài)
  - Owner's Equity = 所有者权益 (suǒyǒuzhě quányì)
  - Revenue = 收入 (shōurù)
  - Expense = 费用 (fèiyòng)
  - Capital Expenditure = 资本支出 (zīběn zhīchū)
  - Operating Expense = 经营费用 (jīngyíng fèiyòng)

### Advanced Extension
- Explore why SMEs might simplify accounting treatments compared to large corporations (e.g., tax vs. GAAP requirements)
- Investigate how Hong Kong's GST-free environment affects SME accounting compared to countries with VAT/GST

### Support for Struggling Students
- Step-by-step worked examples with visual diagrams
- Scaffolded practice problems with increasing complexity
- Peer tutoring during group activities
