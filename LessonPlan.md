# Lesson Plan: Curriculum Design
**Instructor**: [Your Name] | **Date**: [Insert Date]

---

## 1. Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)
By the end of the lesson, students will be able to:
1. **Analyze** foundational principles of curriculum design and their impact on educational outcomes.
2. **Evaluate** different curriculum models (e.g., traditional, learner-centered, standards-based) against predefined criteria.
3. **Design** a curriculum framework for a hypothetical secondary subject area that aligns with learning theories, goals, and assessment standards.

---

## 2. Pre-Class Preparation
**Materials**:
- **Pre-reading**: "Understanding Curriculum Development" (Smith, 2020) [Link](https://example.com/curriculum-reading).
- **Video**: [YouTube: *Curriculum Design Models Explained*](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ABC123).
- **Pre-test**: 8-question quiz on Moodle (e.g., "Define 'backward design model' and its implications for assessment").

**Guiding Questions**:
1. What factors influence curriculum design in your previous educational experiences?
2. Can you identify strengths and weaknesses of learner-centered models?
3. How might cultural context affect curriculum priorities?

---

## 3. Teaching & Learning Activities

### **Introduction (15 minutes)**
**Hook**: Present a scenario: *"You are tasked with redesigning a failing high school curriculum. What are your first steps?"*
**Pre-test Review**:
- Share anonymous word cloud results from pre-test responses to identify common misconceptions (e.g., confusion between instructional models and assessment frameworks).
**Connection to Prior Learning**: Link to prior session on learning theories (e.g., "How does constructivism influence curriculum design?").

---

### **Development Activities (90 minutes)**

#### **Interactive Lecture & Think-Pair-Share (20 minutes)**
- **Topic**: Overview of curriculum design frameworks (traditional vs. backward design vs. competency-based models).
- **Think-Pair-Share Prompt**: *Which model best supports inclusive education? Why?*
- **Visual Aid**: Animated flowchart comparing stages of backward design (Wiggins & McTighe) with traditional models.

#### **Group Case Study Analysis (30 minutes)**
**Activity**: [Group Discussion]
- **Case Study**: *A rural high school seeks to integrate STEM into its curriculum but lacks resources.*
  - **Guided Questions**:
    1. What contextual factors (e.g., funding, community needs) influence this curriculum redesign?
    2. Propose two alignment strategies between standards and classroom practice.
- **Scaffolding**: Provide a **graphic organizer** outlining the DACUM (Developing A CUrriculum) method to structure analysis.

#### **Curriculum Framework Design (30 minutes)**
**Activity**: [Group Work]
- **Task**: Design a 4-week micro-curriculum for a secondary subject (e.g., biology, history) that:
  - Aligns with Bloom's Taxonomy.
  - Incorporates formative and summative assessments.
  - Addresses diversity (e.g., multilingual learners).
- **Tools**: Digital whiteboard (Jamboard) or physical poster paper for sketches.
- **Gallery Walk**: Groups present frameworks to the class and receive peer feedback via sticky notes.

#### **Formative Quiz (10 minutes)**
- **Platform**: Mentimeter poll with questions like: *Which model prioritizes learning outcomes first?*
- **Feedback**: Display anonymized response trends (e.g., 70% correct answers to "What is the purpose of curriculum mapping?").

---

### **Synthesis & Closure (15 minutes)**
- **Post-Test**: Mirror 3 questions from pre-test to assess learning gains.
- **Reflection**: *One-minute paper* on "One challenge I foresee in curriculum design and how to overcome it."
- **Preview**: Next session on assessment alignment—students should explore a real-world curriculum example (e.g., IB diploma programme).

---

## 4. Assessment Methods

### **Formative**
- **Pre-/Post-Test Comparison**: Measure knowledge shifts (e.g., understanding of backward design).
- **Peer Feedback**: Groups use a checklist to evaluate each other's frameworks.
- **Exit Tickets**: Themes from one-minute papers.

### **Summative (Due One Week Later)**
- **Curriculum Proposal**: 1500-word document outlining a semester-long curriculum for a subject of choice, including:
  - Aligned learning objectives (using SMART criteria).
  - Assessment rubrics and instructional strategies.
- **Rubric**:
  - **40%**: Clarity and alignment of objectives, instruction, and assessment.
  - **30%**: Incorporation of equity, diversity, and cultural relevance.
  - **30%**: Theoretical justification (e.g., why Bloom's Taxonomy is appropriate).

## 7. Differentiation & Inclusivity
- **Support**: Provide audio summaries of written materials for auditory learners.
- **Extension**: Advanced students explore UNESCO's *Global Citizenship Education* framework.
- **Accessibility**: Use alt-text on slides, ensure group roles accommodate all participation styles.

---

## 8. Reflection & Improvement
- **Success Indicators**: High engagement in group discussions and quality of framework designs.
- **Feedback**: Collect student surveys on LMS; track summative proposal grades.
- **Future Modifications**: Add a guest speaker (curriculum director) for real-world insights.
