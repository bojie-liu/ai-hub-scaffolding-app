### 1. Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1. Analyze the key components of the Hong Kong EFL curriculum framework (Bloom's: Analyze)
2. Evaluate pedagogical strategies aligned with the biliteracy-trilingualism policy (Bloom's: Evaluate)
3. Adapt curriculum standards to context-specific lesson planning (Bloom's: Create)
4. Critically reflect on the integration of CLIL (Content and Language Integrated Learning) approaches (Bloom's: Analyze/Evaluate)

---

### 2. Pre-Class Preparation
**Pre-Reading**:
- Hong Kong Education Bureau (HKEB). (2020). *English Language Curriculum Guide (Primary 1 – Secondary 6)* (Excerpts on biliteracy policy and CLIL, pp.12-25)
- Short video: [YouTube: "CLIL Strategies in Hong Kong Schools"](https://example.com/clilhk)

**Pre-Test** (Google Forms, 5 questions):
- Example question: [MCQ] "Which of the following best represents Hong Kong's biliteracy goal? (A) Proficiency in English and Mandarin (B) Proficiency in Cantonese and English (C) Mastery of academic writing in English"
- Open-response: "Explain the challenges of implementing CLIL in Hong Kong's multilingual environment."

**Guiding Questions**:
- "How does the Hong Kong curriculum address the interplay between English and local languages?"
- "What are the implications of the 'three-year flexible timeframe' for EFL syllabus design?"

---

### 3. Teaching & Learning Activities

#### **Introduction (12 minutes)**
- **Hook**: Analyze a controversial news headline: "Is Hong Kong's English curriculum failing native speakers?"
- **Pre-Test Discussion**: Use word cloud visualization (Mentimeter) to compare class responses, highlighting misconceptions like conflating biliteracy with bilingualism
- **Real-World Connection**: Showcase HKET (Hong Kong Examinations Authority) 2023 data about declining secondary-level EFL performance

#### **Development Activities (62 minutes)**

**A. Jigsaw Group Expertise Building (15 minutes)**
1. Divide class into 5 expert groups (6 students each):
   - Group 1: Biliteracy Policy
   - Group 2: CLIL Implementation
   - Group 3: Four Key Strands Framework
   - Group 4: Assessment Literacy (HKDSE Exam Alignment)
   - Group 5: Technology Integration (EdCity Platform case study)
2. Each group analyzes assigned curriculum documents with provided graphic organizer

**B. Group Problem-Solving (20 minutes)**
- **Case Study**: Create a mock school budget proposal meeting curriculum requirements within pandemic-era constraints
- **Scaffolding**: Provide worked example showing CLIL integration in Primary Science class using EdCity resources

**C. Peer Teaching Rounds (12 minutes)**
- Groups develop 3-minute "TED-style" presentations with visual aids (Canva templates provided)
- Cross-group feedback using criteria: Clarity of policy interpretation / Feasibility of implementation

**D. Concept Mapping Challenge (10 minutes)**
- Use Padlet to create a collaborative concept map linking curriculum components
- Example addition: "Policy: Three-Year Flexible Timeframe → Strategy: Modular Scheduling → Assessment: Portfolio System"

**E. Formative Assessment (5 minutes)**
- Lightning quiz (Mentimeter): "Identify 2 challenges in implementing CLIL in Hong Kong" (Text response)

Break (5 minutes)

#### **Synthesis & Closure (16 minutes)**
- **Post-Test**: Compare pre/post-test scores using LMS analytics
- **Reflective Discussion**: "How might Hong Kong's curriculum approach influence our own teaching context?"
- **Preview**: Introduce next session's focus on differentiated instruction within curriculum constraints

---

### 4. Assessment Methods

#### **Formative Assessment**
- Padlet concept map contributions (tracked via timestamps)
- Mentimeter quiz responses (anonymized analytics)
- Peer teaching observation checklist (rubric provided to students)

#### **Summative Assessment**
- **Group Presentation**: 15-minute podcast episode critiquing HK curriculum (rubric: Policy Analysis 40%, Practical Application 30%, Creativity 30%)
- **Individual Assignment**: 800-word policy adaptation essay (rubric: Critical Thinking 50%, Contextualization 30%, Referencing 20%)
