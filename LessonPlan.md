# Lesson Plan: Introduction to Learning Theories
**Duration**: 90 minutes
**Class Size**: 43 students
**Subject**: Education
**Topic**: Introduction to Learning Theories
**Language**: English

---

## 1. Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)
By the end of this lesson, students will be able to:
1. **Analyze** key principles of behaviorism, cognitivism, and constructivism using academic terminology.
2. **Compare** strengths and limitations of three learning theories through real-world teaching scenarios.
3. **Apply** one learning theory to design a mini-lesson plan activity that aligns with specific educational goals.

---

## 2. Pre-Class Preparation
**Reading Material**:
- OpenStax *Educational Psychology* textbook (Chapter 4: "Foundations of Learning Theories") [Read sections 4.1–4.3]

**Pre-Test (5 questions)**:
1. What is the role of reinforcement in learning?
2. What is the difference between intrinsic and extrinsic motivation?
3. Define schema theory.
4. Name one limitation of behaviorism.
5. How does prior knowledge influence learning?

**Guiding Questions**:
- How might a teacher apply behaviorism in classroom management?
- Can you recall a learning experience that felt more "cognitive" than "behavioral"?

---

## 3. Teaching & Learning Activities

### **Introduction (10 minutes)**
- **Hook**: Project an image of a teacher giving a student a gold star for sitting quietly. Ask:
  → "What learning theory does this visualize? Why might this work or fail?"
- **Pre-Test Discussion**: Use Mentimeter live word cloud to display responses. Address misconceptions (e.g., equating behaviorism solely with rewards).
- **Real-World Connection**: Show a 2-minute video snippet of a classroom using gamified learning (e.g., Classcraft or Kahoot!).

---

### **Development Activities (65 minutes)**

1. **Interactive Mini-Lecture (15 minutes)**:
   - Explain behaviorism (Skinner), cognitivism (Piaget), and constructivism (Vygotsky) using an animated infographic.
   - **Think-Pair-Share**: "Which theory best explains how you learned to ride a bike?"

2. **Small Group Case Study Analysis (25 minutes)**:
   - Divide students into 6 groups (7–8 members). Each group receives a unique case:
     - Case A: Teaching multiplication to 3rd graders (behaviorism)
     - Case B: High school dropout reengagement program (cognitivism)
     - Case C: Engineering design competition for college students (constructivism)
   - **Task**:
     i) Identify the dominant learning theory
     ii) Propose two teaching strategies for the case
     iii) Sketch a 3-step lesson plan fragment

3. **Digital Jigsaw Activity (15 minutes)**:
   - Regroup students into "theory expert" clusters (e.g., all original behaviorism groups meet).
   - Use Padlet to share and critique group solutions.

4. **Flashcard Challenge (10 minutes)**:
   - Quizlet Live game with 10 terms (e.g., "operant conditioning," "scaffolding"). Groups compete to match terms with definitions.

---

### **Synthesis & Closure (15 minutes)**
- **Post-Test**: Repeat 2 pre-test questions and add:
  → "Design a classroom activity using your assigned learning theory."
- **Reflection Circle**: Students share one takeaway and one unanswered question via Mentimeter polls.
- **Preview**: Introduce next session on social learning theory and technology integration.

---

## 4. Assessment Methods

### **Formative Assessment**
- Observation of group dynamics and solution quality
- Padlet contributions and Quizlet Live scores
- Exit ticket: "Rate your confidence in applying theories (1–5)"

### **Summative Assessment**
- **Written Assignment** (Due next session): Create a 1-hour lesson plan for a K–12 grade of choice, explicitly aligning objectives/activities with one learning theory.
  **Rubric**:
  - Theory selection justification (30%)
  - Activity alignment with theory principles (40%)
  - Practical applicability for teachers (30%)

---

## 6. Required Resources & Technology
- LMS (Canvas/Moodle) for pre/post-tests
- Mentimeter and Padlet accounts
- Projector and laptop
- Printer for graphic organizers
- Quizlet Live access
- Printed case studies (6 x 2 pages each)

---

## 7. Differentiation & Inclusivity
- Provide graphic organizers for all students to structure case analysis
- Offer audio recordings of mini-lecture for auditory learners
- Assign one advanced student per group as "theory coach" to support peers
- Allow alternative format submissions for summative assessment (audio/video/visual learners)

---

## 8. Reflection & Improvement
- **Success Indicators**: At least 80% of groups demonstrate accurate theory alignment in solutions; pre/post-average improvement ≥ 20%.
- **Feedback Mechanisms**: Post-class survey via Google Forms (5 questions).
- **Modifications**: Reduce pre-test questions if pacing is tight; add video examples of theory failures as learning opportunities.
