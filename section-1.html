📚 Student View — focus on activities and learning tasks

Intended Learning Outcomes

Social Constructivism alignment + AI literacy

Bloom's Taxonomy Levels Used in This Lesson
  • Compare → Analyse (Level 4)
  • Apply → Apply (Level 3)
  • Critically evaluate → Evaluate (Level 5)
  • Generate → Create (Level 6)
  • Reflect → Evaluate / Create (Levels 5–6)

Learning Outcomes

ILO 1 Analyse — Bloom's L4

Students will be able to compare and contrast the key assumptions and classroom implications of Behaviourism, Cognitivism, and Social Constructivism.

Pedagogy link: Collaborative theory-mapping mirrors Social Constructivist knowledge co-construction.

AI integration: —

ILO 2 Apply — Bloom's L3

Students will be able to apply at least one learning theory to a specific real-world teaching scenario, justifying their choice.

Pedagogy link: Jigsaw peer-teaching embeds application through explanation and analogy construction.

AI integration: —

ILO 3 Evaluate — Bloom's L5

Students will be able to critically evaluate AI-generated explanations of learning theories, identifying inaccuracies, oversimplifications, and missing nuance.

Pedagogy link: Socratic questioning during debrief scaffolds evaluative thinking in a social context.

🤖 AI Literacy Dimension

Students interrogate a ChatGPT output containing a deliberate error and document what they accepted, modified, or rejected — developing critical AI fluency.

ILO 4 Create — Bloom's L6

Students will be able to generate an AI-assisted analogy for a learning theory and justify why it is or is not pedagogically accurate.

Pedagogy link: ZPD-informed scaffolding — AI provides a draft; students reconstruct it with disciplinary knowledge.

🤖 AI Literacy Dimension

Using AI as a creative scaffold rather than a final source: students produce then evaluate, not just consume.

ILO 5 Evaluate/Create — Bloom's L5–6

Students will be able to reflect on their own use of AI tools during the lesson, articulating one concrete change to their future AI practices.

Pedagogy link: Metacognitive closure; reflective journals align with Social Constructivist emphasis on meaning-making through dialogue and self-assessment.

🤖 AI Literacy Dimension

Metacognitive AI awareness: students move from using AI as a search engine to understanding its limitations, biases, and appropriate role in academic work.
