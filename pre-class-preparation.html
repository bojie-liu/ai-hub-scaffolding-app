📚 Pre-Class Preparation

This is a light flipped-readiness component to activate prior knowledge before the in-class session.

⏱️ Estimated Time: 30–40 minutes

Complete these activities before attending the 120-minute session.

Required Reading

📖 Gibbons, P. (2002). Scaffolding Language, Scaffolding Learning, Chapter 2

Focus: Teacher talk and scaffolding in content-based instruction

Key concepts to notice:

  • How teachers use language to scaffold student learning
  • Examples of scaffolded teacher talk
  • The difference between simplifying and scaffolding

Required Video

🎥 Reading Rockets: "Think-Aloud Strategy"

Duration: ~5 minutes

Link: https://www.readingrockets.org/strategies/think_aloud

What to watch for:

  • How the teacher verbalizes their thinking while reading
  • Which reading strategies are demonstrated
  • How students respond to the modelling

Diagnostic Quiz

Complete this 5-question quiz on Moodle before class. Adaptive feedback will be provided after each answer.

Teacher Note: Use quiz data to form mixed-ability groups before the session.

Question 1: Zone of Proximal Development

Which statement best describes Vygotsky's Zone of Proximal Development?

A. The range of tasks a learner can complete independently
B. The distance between what a learner can do alone and what they can do with guidance
C. The cognitive development stage appropriate for a learner's age
D. The amount of time needed for a learner to master a skill
Correct! The ZPD is the distance between independent performance and assisted performance. This is where scaffolding is most effective.

Question 2: Purpose of Think-Aloud

What is the main purpose of a think-aloud?

A. To test students' comprehension after reading
B. To make invisible reading strategies visible and explicit
C. To simplify the text for struggling readers
D. To encourage students to read aloud in class
Correct! Think-alouds reveal the cognitive processes expert readers use automatically, making them accessible for learners.

Question 3: Annotation Symbols

Which annotation symbol might a reader use to show a personal connection to the text?

A. ? (question mark)
B. * (star)
C. C (connection)
D. ! (exclamation)
Correct! The "C" symbol represents connections: text-to-self, text-to-text, or text-to-world.

Question 4: Scaffolding vs. Simplifying

"Scaffolding" in reading instruction means:

A. Permanently reducing text difficulty for struggling readers
B. Providing temporary support that is gradually removed
C. Avoiding challenging texts until students are ready
D. Giving students the answers when they ask questions
Correct! Scaffolding is temporary, strategic support that fades as learners gain independence—not permanent simplification.

Question 5: Reading Strategies

Which of the following is a reading strategy an expert reader might verbalize during a think-aloud?

A. "I'm predicting what will happen next based on this clue..."
B. "This word has three syllables."
C. "The author uses a metaphor here."
D. "This text is written at a Grade 8 level."
Correct! Prediction is a comprehension strategy. Option B focuses on decoding, not comprehension; C names a device without showing thinking; D is meta-analysis, not active reading.

Guiding Questions for Reflection

As you complete the readings and video, consider these questions. Bring your reflections to class:

  1. Why is teacher modelling an effective way to teach reading strategies?
  2. What role do peers play in supporting reading comprehension?
  3. Notice one example of scaffolded teacher talk from the Gibbons reading. What made it effective?

Teacher Preparation Notes

  • Review quiz results before class to identify common misconceptions
  • Use quiz data to create 15–18 mixed-ability groups (5–6 students each)
  • Prepare Text A (for teacher think-aloud modelling) and Text B (for group annotation)
  • Set up Mentimeter polls and Padlet board with QR codes
  • Print annotation code cards and role cards for each group
  • Brief TAs on scaffolding questions for group circulation
