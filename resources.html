Key Readings & References

Book

Mind in Society

Vygotsky, L.S. (1978). Harvard University Press.

Foundational text for Social Constructivism. Introduces the Zone of Proximal Development, scaffolding, and the social origins of higher mental functions.

Essential for understanding ZPD and social constructivism.
Book

The Behavior of Organisms

Skinner, B.F. (1938). Appleton-Century-Crofts.

The seminal work establishing operant conditioning. Skinner's experimental framework for reinforcement and punishment remains foundational to behaviourism.

Core reading for behaviourist theory and operant conditioning.
Book

Toward a Theory of Instruction

Bruner, J. (1966). Harvard University Press.

Bridges cognitivism and constructivism. Explores spiral curriculum, discovery learning, and the role of readiness in education.

Connects cognitivist and constructivist principles.
Book

Educational Psychology: A Cognitive View

Ausubel, D.P. (1968).

Introduces meaningful learning theory and advance organisers. Ausubel's model explains how new knowledge is integrated into existing cognitive structures.

Key text for understanding advance organisers and schema.
Article

Cognitive Load During Problem Solving

Sweller, J. (1988). Cognitive Science, 12(2), 257–285.

The original paper proposing cognitive load theory. Establishes the limits of working memory and implications for instructional design.

Foundational for cognitive load and instructional sequencing.
Book

Teaching for Quality Learning at University (4th ed.)

Biggs, J. & Tang, C. (2011). McGraw-Hill.

Introduces constructive alignment — aligning intended learning outcomes, teaching activities, and assessments into a coherent framework.

Directly supports ILO and assessment design in this lesson.

Common AI Limitations for Educators

  • Hallucination — confident but false output
  • Training data cutoff — outdated information
  • Confirmation bias in training data
  • Lack of context about specific courses
  • Oversimplification of complex theories
  • Cultural and linguistic bias
  • Confident errors with no uncertainty markers

Glossary of Key Terms

Behaviourism
Theory that learning is a change in observable behaviour through conditioning — specifically stimulus–response associations shaped by reinforcement and punishment.
Cognitivism
Theory focused on internal mental processes including memory, attention, schema formation, and cognitive load. Learners process information like a sophisticated system.
Constructivism
Theory that learners actively construct knowledge through experience, drawing on prior understanding and context. Knowledge is built, not passively received.
Social Constructivism
Vygotsky's view that knowledge is built through social interaction, dialogue, and collaboration. Learning happens first between people, then within the individual.
Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD)
The gap between what a learner can achieve independently and what they can achieve with guidance or in collaboration with more capable peers.
Scaffolding
Temporary, adjustable support provided to help learners reach the next level of understanding or skill — gradually removed as competence grows.
Cognitive Load
The mental effort required to process new information. Working memory is limited; instructional design should manage intrinsic, extraneous, and germane load.
Constructive Alignment
Biggs' framework aligning Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs), teaching and learning activities, and assessment tasks into a coherent, mutually reinforcing system.
AI Hallucination
When an AI model generates plausible-sounding but factually incorrect or fabricated content, often without any indication of uncertainty.
Formative Assessment
Ongoing assessment integrated into the learning process to provide feedback and guide next steps — contrasted with summative assessment used for grading.
