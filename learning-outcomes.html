📋 Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)

By the end of this session, students will be able to:

ILO 1: Apply Think-Aloud Protocol

Apply a think-aloud protocol to make expert reading strategies visible and accessible for L2 readers.

Application

Assessment: Teacher observation during modelling; exit ticket naming strategies

ILO 2: Collaborative Annotation

Use collaborative annotation codes and discussion prompts to co-construct meaning from a text with peers.

Application

Assessment: Padlet gallery; teacher observation checklist

ILO 3: Analyze ZPD Scaffolding

Analyze how teacher and peer scaffolding can move learners through the Zone of Proximal Development during reading tasks.

Analysis

Assessment: Exit ticket reflection on peer support; discussion participation

ILO 4: Evaluate Scaffolding Tools

Evaluate the use of various scaffolding tools for diverse secondary learners in English language classrooms.

Evaluation

Assessment: Summative rubric criterion on differentiation

ILO 5: Design Mini-Lesson

Design a short mini-lesson segment integrating think-aloud modelling and guided group annotation for a secondary reading text.

Creation

Assessment: Summative mini-lesson plan with rubric

Bloom's Taxonomy Alignment

These learning outcomes span multiple levels of Bloom's Taxonomy, from application through creation:

  • Application (ILO 1, 2): Using strategies in practical contexts
  • Analysis (ILO 3): Examining how scaffolding functions within ZPD
  • Evaluation (ILO 4): Judging the effectiveness of different tools
  • Creation (ILO 5): Designing original instructional materials

Connection to Professional Practice

These outcomes prepare you for authentic teaching scenarios in Hong Kong secondary English classrooms:

Why Think-Aloud Matters +

Think-aloud protocols make invisible cognitive processes visible. When you model your reading process, you demonstrate strategies that expert readers use automatically but struggling readers need to learn explicitly.

In practice: You'll use think-alouds when introducing complex texts, teaching inference, or supporting ESL learners.

Why Collaborative Annotation Matters +

Annotation codes provide a shared language for discussing texts. Group annotation leverages peer scaffolding—students in their own ZPD support each other through dialogue.

In practice: You'll use collaborative annotation for literature circles, close reading tasks, and differentiated instruction.

Why ZPD Analysis Matters +

Understanding ZPD helps you calibrate support—neither too much (dependence) nor too little (frustration). You learn when to scaffold and when to fade support.

In practice: You'll apply ZPD thinking to lesson planning, assessment design, and responsive teaching.
