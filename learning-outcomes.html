Intended Learning Outcomes (ILOs)

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

1. Analyze Characteristics of Teacher Professionalism

Bloom's Level: Analyze

Analyze the defining characteristics of teacher professionalism in at least two different cultural/institutional contexts.

Assessment: Jigsaw expert groups, Jamboard creation, post-test

2. Compare and Contrast Frameworks

Bloom's Level: Evaluate

Compare and contrast local (e.g., Hong Kong/Singapore) and international (e.g., Finland/USA) frameworks of teacher professionalism, identifying sociocultural and policy influences.

Assessment: Gallery walk sticky notes, case study recommendation

3. Construct a Shared Conceptual Map

Bloom's Level: Create

Construct a shared conceptual map that integrates multiple perspectives on teacher professionalism, using peer dialogue and evidence.

Assessment: Collaborative concept map, individual contributions

4. Critique a Professionalism Dilemma

Bloom's Level: Evaluate

Critique a case study of a professionalism dilemma, applying both local and international norms.

Assessment: Written recommendation paragraph, oral sharing

5. Reflect on Teaching Identity

Bloom's Level: Metacognition

Reflect on how one's own (or future) teaching identity is shaped by contextual expectations.

Assessment: Exit ticket, post-test reflection, final essay

🎓 Teaching Notes

Pedagogical Approach: Social Constructivism

All ILOs depend on collaborative discussion, shared meaning-making, and peer scaffolding. Students build knowledge together rather than receiving it passively.

Key Principles:

  • Collaborative knowledge-building: Students negotiate meaning through discourse
  • Contextual comparison: Multiple perspectives are integrated, not ranked
  • Authentic problems: Case studies connect theory to practice
  • Peer scaffolding: Students support each other's learning
