Pedagogy-Specific Success Indicators

💬 Quality of Peer Dialogue

Social Constructivism

Observable indicators during jigsaw and group mapping:

  • Number of questions asked between peers
  • Use of evidence from readings to support claims
  • Students building on each other's contributions

🧩 Degree of Co-Construction

Social Constructivism

Group comparison charts show:

  • Shared contributions from multiple group members
  • Revisions made collaboratively (not one person's work)
  • Evidence of negotiated meaning in language used

🎓 Confidence in Teaching Peers

Self-Report

Captured via exit ticket self-report:

  • Students rate their confidence explaining their assigned theory
  • Peer evaluators note clarity and accuracy
  • Instructor tracks change from pre-class to post-activity

Modification Strategies

Use these targeted adjustments based on what you observe during or after the lesson.

🔍 Hallucination Hunt

If students over-trust AI

Add a dedicated activity where groups are given an AI output known to contain factual errors and tasked specifically with finding them. Celebrating the act of correction shifts students' relationship to AI output.

🧱 Structured Prompts

If students under-engage with AI

Reduce open-endedness: provide specific prompts for students to copy verbatim, then evaluate the output. Reduces the cognitive load of prompt design and focuses attention on critique.

🎯 Theory Focus

If mapping takes too long

Assign one theory per group rather than all three. Groups become true experts in a single theory, then share out during the jigsaw. Reduces coverage breadth but deepens engagement.

📋 Peer Feedback Forms

If peer dialogue lacks depth

Introduce structured peer feedback forms with specific prompts: "Name one strength of the explanation," "Identify one inaccuracy or gap," "Ask one clarifying question." More structure deepens critical dialogue.
