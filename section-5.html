Section 3c — Synthesis & Closure

PSY101 · 105–120 minutes · 12% of lesson time · Consolidation + AI Reflection
🎯 Closure Purpose

This phase consolidates learning, surfaces remaining misconceptions, and explicitly reflects on AI integration — establishing the expectation that AI use requires transparency, critical evaluation, and documented human judgment.

⏱Closure Timeline (105–120 min)
105–108 min
📋 Post-Test Quick Check (3 questions)

Instructor administers a 3-question quiz via Mentimeter or Poll Everywhere. Results are displayed immediately.

  • Q1: Identify the CS and CR in a new scenario (never seen before).
  • Q2: Distinguish positive reinforcement vs. negative reinforcement in a workplace example.
  • Q3: Apply observational learning to explain why a child picks up slang from peers.

Students compare answers in pairs and correct each other’s errors before the instructor clarifies.

Collaborative meta-cognition

Social Constructivism: Peer checking surfaces discrepancies; students learn by explaining their reasoning to a partner within the ZPD.

108–112 min
🪞 Peer Teaching Reflection

Instructor asks the class:

  • "Which slogan or metaphor from another group would you steal? Why?"
  • "Which theory do you now feel MOST confident about?"
  • "What still feels unclear?"

Students share verbally or post to the Padlet reflection wall.

🤖 AI Reflection Discussion
  • "Where did AI help your group today?"
  • "Where did AI get in the way?"
  • "Did any group feel the AI led them astray? What decisions did YOU need to override?"

AI Integration: Students explicitly name their human override decisions — building metacognitive AI literacy vocabulary.

Social Constructivism: Collaborative metacognition — students verbally articulate their own cognitive state and learn from peers' AI encounters.

112–115 min
🔭 Preview & Next Steps

Instructor connects current learning to next week's topic: Cognitive Learning Theories (memory and information processing).

Instructor introduces the upcoming major assessment (Learning Theory Portfolio) and emphasises:

"Your portfolio will include AI use documentation — you'll need to show me not just WHAT you learned, but HOW you made decisions with and against AI."
Portfolio introduced
AI transparency expectation set

Frames future learning as a continued collaborative process — peers will provide portfolio feedback in Week 2.

Answer all three questions. Your instructor may review these to adjust next week's session.

  1. One-word summary: What single word captures your biggest takeaway from today?
  2. Continuing question: What is one question you still have about learning theories?
  3. AI reflection: Did AI help you learn today? Why or why not? (2–3 sentences)
Submit via Mentimeter
🎤 Instructor Closing Script
"Today you engaged with three key theories. And you also learned something about AI. You learned that it's a powerful tool — but you are the expert. Your judgment is essential. In your portfolio, you'll need to document this judgment. Show me where you agreed with AI, where you challenged it, and where you overturned it — and why. That's what we mean by becoming an AI-literate psychologist."
